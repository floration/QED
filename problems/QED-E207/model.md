# QED-E207 — Strong shift equivalence of the Baker matrices for every k

You are an AI model helping someone attack this open problem. Read all of this before working.

## The problem
Extended set · Grade B · QED Curated · Dynamics

**Plain words:** For each k >= 1, the 2x2 matrices A_k = [[1, k+1],[k, 1]] and B_k = [[1, k(k+1)],[1, 1]] are similar over the integers and primitive, so their shifts of finite type are shift equivalent. Are the two shifts topologically conjugate for every k, i.e. can A_k be turned into B_k by finitely many moves RS -> SR with nonnegative integer matrices R, S?

**Math:** Let $A_k=\begin{pmatrix}1&k+1\\k&1\end{pmatrix}$, $B_k=\begin{pmatrix}1&k(k+1)\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$, and let $\sim_{SSE}$ be the equivalence relation on square nonnegative integer matrices of all sizes generated by $RS\approx SR$ ($R,S$ rectangular, nonnegative integer). Determine whether $A_k\sim_{SSE}B_k$ for every integer $k\ge1$.

**Credit:** K. A. Baker, Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 3 (1983); recent computations E. Jeandel arXiv:2609.03567.

**Rests on recent unrefereed work. Check these premises first:**
- Small-k results for 3<=k<=12 rest on an unrefereed Sept 2026 preprint (Jeandel), which also contains an internally inconsistent sentence ('open for k>=3'); the claim for all k does not depend on them
- Overlaps thematically with the SSE-decidability candidate, but is a distinct statement (a decision procedure would not by itself settle all k)
