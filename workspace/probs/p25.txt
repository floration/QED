## Real zeros of the theta cosine sum | 6 | Analysis
W: Count the zeros of cos(x) + cos(4x) + cos(9x) + ... + cos(N²x) over one period. A random-sign version has about 0.894 N² zeros, but the true theta sum seems to have about 0.93 N². Prove the count divided by N² has a limit and that it beats the random prediction.
M: Let $Z_N=\#\{x\in[0,2\pi):\sum_{n=1}^N\cos(n^2x)=0\}$. Prove that $\lim_{N\to\infty}Z_N/N^2=c$ exists and that $c>2/\sqrt5$ (the Kac–Rice value for random signs $\pm\cos(n^2x)$). Grid sign-change counts (FFT, 24–64 points per shortest period) give $Z_N/N^2=0.9379,0.9334,0.9327,0.9315$ for $N=160,320,640,1280$, while random signs give $0.8954$ at $N=640$.

## Real zeros of the prime cosine sum | 6 | Analysis
W: Count the zeros of the sum of cos(px) over primes p below X, over one period. Prove the count matches what random signs would predict, about twice the root-mean-square prime, even though the sum has huge spikes near rationals with small denominators.
M: Let $Z(X)=\#\{x\in[0,2\pi):\sum_{p<X}\cos(px)=0\}$ and $R(X)=2\big(\pi(X)^{-1}\sum_{p<X}p^2\big)^{1/2}\sim 2X/\sqrt3$. Prove $Z(X)/R(X)\to1$. Grid counts give $Z(X)/R(X)=1.126,1.089,1.065$ for $X=10^4,10^5,10^6$ (random signs give $1.0003$ at $X=10^6$), so the convergence, if it holds, is slow and from above; determine the rate.

## Zeros of the theta polynomial inside the disc | 6 | Analysis
W: The polynomial 1 + z + z⁴ + z⁹ + ... + z^(N²) has N² roots, nearly all close to the unit circle. With random signs, one third of them lie inside the circle on average, but for the theta polynomial itself the fraction seems to settle near 0.346. Prove the fraction has a limit and find it.
M: Let $F_N(z)=\sum_{n=0}^N z^{n^2}$ and $I_N=\#\{z:|z|<1,F_N(z)=0\}$. Prove $I_N/N^2\to c$ and identify $c$ (for instance as an integral against the limiting distribution of normalized theta sums $N^{-1/2}\sum_{n\le N}e(n^2x+ny)$). Winding numbers computed on 32 points per shortest period give $I_N/N^2=0.3432,0.3440,0.3460,0.34646,0.34628$ for $N=50,100,200,400,800$; with independent random signs the expected fraction is $1/3$ (observed $0.333\pm0.001$ at $N=400$). Prove in particular that $c>1/3$.

## Chaos in the Reuleaux triangle billiard | 8 | Dynamics
W: A ball bouncing inside a Reuleaux triangle (the rounded triangle of constant width) has a stable three-bounce orbit hitting the middles of the three arcs, so the motion is not fully chaotic. Prove that the chaotic part nevertheless fills a region of positive area in phase space.
M: Let $R$ be the Reuleaux triangle of width 1 and $T$ its billiard map on $\partial R\times(0,\pi)$ with the invariant measure $\sin\phi\,ds\,d\phi$. The 3-periodic orbit through the arc midpoints has monodromy trace $\approx0.8986$ (computed numerically), so it is elliptic. Prove that $T$ has positive metric entropy, i.e. a positive-measure set of points with a nonzero Lyapunov exponent.
## Graph dimension of the prime Riemann function | 6 | Analysis
W: Riemann's function sums sin(n²x)/n² and has a fractal graph of dimension 5/4. Do the same over primes: add sin(px)/p^s over all primes p. Show the graph's Hausdorff dimension is 5/2 − s, exactly what random-phase heuristics predict, for every s between 1 and 3/2.
M: For $1<s<\tfrac32$ let $f_s(x)=\sum_{p}p^{-s}\sin(px)$ (absolutely and uniformly convergent, hence continuous). Prove $\dim_H\{(x,f_s(x)):x\in[0,2\pi]\}=\tfrac52-s$. (The $L^2$ modulus of continuity $\asymp h^{s-1/2}(\log 1/h)^{-1/2}$ predicts this value; but at each $x=2\pi a/q$ with $\mu(q)\neq0$ the function has a cusp of Hölder exponent only $s-1$ and amplitude about $1/\varphi(q)$, and these cusps contribute at the same order as the bulk at every scale, so both the upper and the lower bound need real work.)

## Chaos in the gravitational circle billiard | 6 | Dynamics
W: A ball moves inside a circular hoop under constant gravity, bouncing elastically off the hoop. At very low energy it is nearly a parabolic gravity billiard and at very high energy nearly the ordinary circle billiard, and both of those are integrable. Prove the motion has positive topological entropy at every energy in between and beyond.
M: Let a unit-mass particle move in the unit disc with potential $V(x,y)=y$, reflecting elastically at $x^2+y^2=1$, at energy $E>-1$ (so $E+1$ is the height reachable above the bottom). Let $T_E$ be the induced billiard map on the boundary section. Prove $h_{\rm top}(T_E)>0$ for every $E>-1$. (Both limits $E\to-1$ (rescaled, the integrable billiard in the parabola $y=x^2/2$ with gravity) and $E\to\infty$ (the circle billiard) are integrable, so the difficulty is uniformity at both ends.)

## Magnetic billiard in a square | 6 | Dynamics
W: A charged particle bounces inside a square while a constant magnetic field bends its path into circular arcs of radius r. With no field the square billiard is integrable. Prove the motion has positive topological entropy for every radius r, however large or small.
M: Let $Q=[0,1]^2$ and consider the magnetic billiard in $Q$: the particle moves at unit speed along circles of radius $r$ (curving to the left) and reflects elastically at $\partial Q$. Prove that for every $r\in(0,\infty)$ the billiard map (on the set where it is defined, away from corners) has a compact invariant set on which it is conjugate to a subshift of positive entropy. Treat the two regimes $r\to\infty$ (perturbation of the square billiard) and $r\to0$ (skipping orbits) and everything between.

## Wind-tree with triangular trees | 6 | Dynamics
W: In the Ehrenfest wind-tree model a particle bounces between identical obstacles placed at every point of the square grid; for square obstacles it spreads like time to the power 2/3. Replace the squares by equilateral triangles, all pointing the same way. Find the spreading exponent for almost every direction and size.
M: For $0<a<1$ let $W_a=\mathbb R^2\setminus\bigcup_{v\in\mathbb Z^2}(v+\Delta_a)$ where $\Delta_a$ is the equilateral triangle of side $a$ with one horizontal side. Billiard directions in $W_a$ take at most 6 values, so the flow lives on a $\mathbb Z^2$-cover of a compact translation surface. Determine $\delta(a)=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log d(\phi_t^\theta(x),x)}{\log t}$ for Lebesgue-a.e. $\theta$ and $x$, for a.e. $a\in(0,1)$, and decide whether it is independent of $a$ (as $2/3$ is for square trees).

## Newton's method for the Riemann xi function | 6 | Dynamics
W: Now that every zero of the Riemann xi function is known to lie on the critical line, Newton's method for xi is a meromorphic map whose target points are all real (after rotating). Decide whether every region where Newton's method behaves stably simply flows into a zero, or whether there are exotic stable regions such as escaping domains or attracting cycles.
M: Let $\Xi(t)=\xi(\tfrac12+it)$, a real entire function of order 1 in the Laguerre–Pólya class, and $N(z)=z-\Xi(z)/\Xi'(z)$. Decide whether the Fatou set of $N$ is exactly the union of the basins of attraction of the zeros of $\Xi$; in particular decide whether $N$ has an invariant Baker domain (a Fatou component on which $N^n\to\infty$), an attracting or parabolic cycle of period at least 2, or a wandering domain.

## The dimension-one contour of the quadratic family | 6 | Dynamics
W: Outside the Mandelbrot set the Julia set of z² + c is a Cantor dust whose fractal dimension drops from at least 1 near the set to 0 far away, and on the real line left of −2 it is always below 1. Decide whether the parameters where the dimension is exactly 1 form a single simple closed curve around the Mandelbrot set, pinched at −2.
M: For $c\notin M$ let $d(c)=\dim_H J(z^2+c)$, a real-analytic function on $\mathbb C\setminus M$ with $d(c)\to0$ as $|c|\to\infty$, $d<1$ on $(-\infty,-2)$, and $d\to1$ as $c\to-2$ along $(-\infty,-2)$. Let $L=\{c\notin M:d(c)=1\}$. Decide whether $L\cup\{-2\}$ is a Jordan curve; decide whether $d$ is strictly decreasing along every external ray of $M$ (which would imply it).
## Random Fibonacci with Legendre signs | 5 | Dynamics
W: Viswanath showed that the random Fibonacci recurrence, where each step adds or subtracts the previous term with a fair coin, grows like 1.13198824 to the n. Replace the coin by the Legendre symbol modulo a large prime p. Prove that over one period the growth rate converges to Viswanath's constant.
M: For an odd prime $p$ let $x_0=x_1=1$ and $x_{n+1}=x_n+\left(\frac np\right)x_{n-1}$ for $1\le n\le p-1$. Prove $\frac1p\log|x_p|\to\log\beta_V$ as $p\to\infty$, where $\beta_V=1.13198824\ldots$ is Viswanath's constant (the almost-sure growth rate of $x_{n+1}=x_n\pm x_{n-1}$ with i.i.d. fair signs); in particular prove $x_p\neq0$ for all large $p$. Computed: $\frac1{p-1}\log|x_p|=0.12553,0.12558,0.12409,0.12414$ for $p=9973,99991,999983,9999991$, against $\log\beta_V=0.12398$. Weil's bound makes sign patterns of length $o(\log p)$ equidistributed, but the products are not uniformly hyperbolic, so this alone does not suffice.

## Density of states on the Sierpinski carpet graph | 7 | Analysis
W: On the Sierpinski gasket graph, the energy levels of the Laplacian are explained by an exact renormalization map and the density of states is made only of jumps. The Sierpinski carpet has no such renormalization. Decide whether its density of states is also purely made of jumps, coming from localized eigenfunctions.
M: Let $G$ be the infinite one-sided Sierpinski carpet graph (the union of the level-$n$ pre-carpet graphs $G_n$ on $8^n$ unit squares, adjacent when they share an edge, nested at a corner) and $\Delta$ its normalized Laplacian. Let $N(\lambda)=\lim_n 8^{-n}\#\{\text{eigenvalues of }\Delta_{G_n}\le\lambda\}$ (Dirichlet or Neumann at the outer boundary). Prove the limit exists and decide whether $N$ is a pure jump function, i.e. whether the eigenvalues with eigenfunctions of compact support on $G$ account for all of the spectral measure.
## Mahler measure of the theta polynomial | 5 | Analysis
W: The polynomial 1 + z + z⁴ + ... + z^(N²) has average square size N + 1 on the unit circle. Its Mahler measure (the geometric mean of its size on the circle) seems to be about 0.75 times the square root of N. Prove the ratio has a limit, and decide whether it equals the value e^(−γ/2) = 0.7493 that random signs would give.
M: Let $F_N(z)=\sum_{n=0}^Nz^{n^2}$ and $M(F_N)=\exp\int_0^1\log|F_N(e(x))|\,dx$. Prove $M(F_N)/\sqrt N\to\kappa$ and decide whether $\kappa=e^{-\gamma/2}$ (the limit for i.i.d. random signs). Since $N^{-1/2}F_N(e(x))$ has a non-Gaussian limiting distribution (Marklof), one expects $\kappa=\exp\mathbb E\log|\Theta|$ for that limit law, and the difficulty is the small values of $|F_N|$. Computed on a 32-points-per-period grid: $M(F_N)/\sqrt{N+1}=0.7533,0.7558,0.7543,0.7529,0.7526$ for $N=50,100,200,400,800$.

## Anderson localization with Legendre-symbol potential | 7 | Analysis
W: On a ring of p sites, put potential +λ or −λ at site n according to whether n is a square modulo the prime p. If the signs were random coin flips, every eigenvector would be exponentially localized. Prove the same for the Legendre-symbol potential as p grows, at every coupling λ > 0.
M: For a prime $p$ and $\lambda>0$ let $H_p=\Delta+\lambda\chi_p$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)$, where $(\Delta u)(n)=u(n+1)+u(n-1)$ and $\chi_p(n)=\left(\frac np\right)$ (with $\chi_p(0)=0$). Prove that there are $c,C>0$ depending only on $\lambda$ such that for all large $p$, all but $o(p)$ normalized eigenvectors $u$ of $H_p$ satisfy $|u(n)|\le Ce^{-c\,\mathrm{dist}(n,n_u)}$ for some centre $n_u$; and that the Lyapunov exponent of the transfer matrices over one period converges, for each energy, to that of the i.i.d. Bernoulli Anderson model.

## Steiner symmetrization in golden-angle directions | 5 | Analysis
W: Repeatedly Steiner-symmetrize a convex shape, each time about a line turned by the golden angle from the previous one. Known examples show that some dense sequences of directions fail to round a shape off. Decide whether this particular sequence always makes the shape converge to a disc.
M: Let $\theta=\pi(3-\sqrt5)$ and let $S_u$ denote Steiner symmetrization with respect to the line through the origin in direction $u$. For a convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ put $K_n=S_{u_n}\cdots S_{u_1}K$ with $u_k=(\cos k\theta,\sin k\theta)$. Decide whether $K_n\to B$ in the Hausdorff metric for every convex body $K$, where $B$ is the centred disc of area $|K|$; if not, characterize the rotation angles $\theta$ for which convergence holds for all $K$.

## The Cantor set meets its square | 5 | Analysis
W: Take the middle-thirds Cantor set C and the set of squares of its points. Their intersection should be small, like the intersection of two unrelated fractals. Prove its dimension is at most 2 dim C − 1 ≈ 0.262, even though both sets live in base 3 and squaring keeps points like 1/9.
M: Let $C$ be the middle-thirds Cantor set, $d=\log2/\log3$, and $C^{(2)}=\{x^2:x\in C\}$. Prove $\dim_H(C\cap C^{(2)})\le 2d-1$. More generally prove $\dim_H(C\cap g(C))\le\max(0,2d-1)$ for every real-analytic diffeomorphism $g$ of $[0,1]$ onto its image that is not affine on any interval. (For affine $g$ such bounds follow from the Shmerkin–Wu theory only for independent bases; here both sets are $\times3$-invariant and the independence must come from the nonlinearity.)
