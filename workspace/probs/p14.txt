## The {5, 8} funnel | 6 | Dynamics
W: Replace n by the sum of the largest prime factors of n and n + 1. Every start tested falls into the loop 5 → 8 → 5. Prove all do.
M: Let $f(n)=P(n)+P(n+1)$ (with $P(1)=1$). Prove every orbit of $f$ on $n\ge2$ enters the cycle $\{5,8\}$.

## Two loops for P(n)+P(n+2) | 6 | Dynamics
W: Replace n by P(n) + P(n + 2). Every tested start ends in 7 → 10 → 8 → 7 or in 9 → 14 → 9. Prove there are no other outcomes.
M: Prove every orbit of $n\mapsto P(n)+P(n+2)$ on $n\ge2$ enters $\{7,10,8\}$ or $\{9,14\}$.

## Doubling the largest factor | 6 | Dynamics
W: Replace n by twice its largest prime factor plus one. Every tested start falls into one loop of eight numbers. Prove all do.
M: Prove every orbit of $n\mapsto 2P(n)+1$ on $n\ge2$ enters the cycle $(7,15,11,23,47,95,39,27)$.

## Doubling minus one | 6 | Dynamics

M: Prove every orbit of $n\mapsto 2P(n)-1$ on $n\ge2$ enters $\{5,9\}$ or $(37,73,145,57)$.

## Three consecutive largest factors | 6 | Dynamics

M: Prove every orbit of $n\mapsto P(n)+P(n+1)+P(n+2)$ on $n\ge2$ enters $\{14\}$ or $(77,103,123)$.

## P(n)+P(n+1)−1 | 6 | Dynamics

M: Prove every orbit of $n\mapsto P(n)+P(n+1)-1$ on $n\ge2$ enters $(4,6,9,7,8)$, $(17,19,23,25)$ or $(167,173,201)$.

## Three halves of the largest factor | 6 | Dynamics

## Two long loops | 7 | Dynamics
W: Replace n by P(3n + 1) + P(n). Every tested start ends in one of two long loops, through 46 or through 80. Prove there are no others.
M: Prove every orbit of $n\mapsto P(3n+1)+P(n)$ on $n\ge2$ enters the 9-cycle through 46 or the 7-cycle through 80.

## Three pairs | 6 | Dynamics
W: Replace n by P(n) + P(2n + 1). Every tested start ends in one of three two-step loops: 13 ↔ 16, 30 ↔ 66, 36 ↔ 76. Prove it.
M: Prove every orbit of $n\mapsto P(n)+P(2n+1)$ on $n\ge2$ enters $\{13,16\}$, $\{30,66\}$ or $\{36,76\}$.

## Neighbours' largest factors | 7 | Dynamics
W: Replace n by P(n − 1) + P(n + 1). Every tested start ends at 5 or in one of three loops, of lengths 11, 18 and 4. Prove there is nothing else.
M: Prove every orbit of $n\mapsto P(n-1)+P(n+1)$ on $n\ge3$ enters $\{5\}$, the 11-cycle through 8, the 18-cycle through 24, or $(224,228,456,470)$.

M: Prove $\#\{n\le x: P(n)\equiv a,\ P(n+1)\equiv b\ (10)\}\sim\frac{x}{16}$ for all $a,b\in\{1,3,7,9\}$.

## Smooth p²+1 | 6 | Number theory

## Linnik–Gallagher with seven-free primes | 6 | Number theory
