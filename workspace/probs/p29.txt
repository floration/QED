## Galois groups of cross-polytope Ehrhart polynomials | 5 | Algebra
W: Count the lattice points in the n-th dilate of the n-dimensional cross-polytope; the answer is a polynomial whose roots all lie on one vertical line. Show that, apart from one exceptional factorization in dimension 6, this polynomial is as irreducible and as asymmetric as its built-in mirror symmetry allows.
M: Let $L_n(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^k\binom nk\binom tk$ be the Ehrhart polynomial of the cross-polytope $\Diamond_n=\mathrm{conv}(\pm e_i)\subset\mathbb R^n$; it satisfies $L_n(-1-t)=(-1)^nL_n(t)$, so $L_n(t)=(2t+1)^{\epsilon}\,g_n\big((2t+1)^2\big)$ with $\epsilon=n\bmod 2$ and $\deg g_n=m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. Prove that for every $n\ne 6$, $g_n$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ with Galois group $S_m$, and that the splitting field of $L_n$ has Galois group the full hyperoctahedral group $C_2\wr S_m$. (Checked: $g_n$ is irreducible for $2\le n\le 60$ except $g_6\propto(s+9)(s^2+46s+25)$; for $n\le 22$ Frobenius cycle types mod primes $<2000$ include an $m$-cycle and the number of distinct types is consistent with $S_m$.) Also show $n=6$ (root $t=-\tfrac12\pm\tfrac32 i$) is the only exceptional $n$, and prove the analogous statement for the type-A root polytope $\mathrm{conv}\{e_i-e_j\}$, whose reduced Ehrhart polynomial is irreducible for all $3\le n\le39$.

## Singularities of the permanental hypersurface | 6 | Algebra
W: The hypersurface where the determinant vanishes has the mildest possible kind of singularities (rational ones), a fact that underlies much of its geometry. Decide whether the hypersurface where the n × n permanent vanishes is equally mild, by computing its log canonical threshold.
M: Let $X_n=\{\operatorname{per}_n=0\}\subset\mathbb A^{n^2}_{\mathbb C}$. For $n=2$ it is a nondegenerate quadric cone in $\mathbb A^4$, with rational singularities. For every $n\ge3$ determine the log canonical threshold $\operatorname{lct}(\mathbb A^{n^2},X_n)$ and Saito's minimal exponent $\tilde\alpha(X_n)$, and decide whether $X_n$ has rational singularities (equivalently $\tilde\alpha(X_n)>1$). In particular decide whether the answer is the same as for $\{\det_n=0\}$ (rational singularities for all $n$) or fails from some $n$ on.

## Normal matrices over finite fields | 4 | Algebra
W: Over the reals a matrix that commutes with its transpose is orthogonally diagonalizable, but over a finite field no such structure theorem exists. Count the n × n matrices over a finite field of odd size that commute with their own transpose.
M: For odd $q$ let $N_n(q)=\#\{A\in M_n(\mathbb F_q): AA^{T}=A^{T}A\}$. Computation gives $N_2(q)=q^3+q^2-q$ ($q=3,5,7,11,13$), $N_3(q)=q^6+q^5-q^2$ ($q=3,5,7$), and $N_4(3)=86913$ (which breaks the naive pattern $q^{10}+q^9-q^3$). Determine $N_n(q)$ for all $n$: decide whether it is a polynomial in $q$ (or a quasi-polynomial depending on $q\bmod 4$), and find a generating function for $\sum_n N_n(q)x^n/|O_n(\mathbb F_q)|$-type sums. Equivalently count commuting pairs $(S,K)$ with $S$ symmetric and $K$ skew-symmetric.

## Pairs with a nilpotent commutator | 5 | Algebra
W: Feit and Fine counted pairs of commuting matrices over a finite field. Count instead the pairs whose commutator is merely nilpotent, a much larger set that still contains every simultaneously triangularizable pair.
M: Let $V_n(q)=\#\{(A,B)\in M_n(\mathbb F_q)^2: (AB-BA)^n=0\}$. Exhaustive counts give $V_2(q)=q^5(q^2+q-1)$ for $q=2,3,5,7$ and $V_3(2)=90112=2^{13}\cdot 11$. Prove that $V_n(q)$ is a polynomial in $q$ for every $n$, and find a closed form or a generating function analogous to the Feit–Fine product $\sum_n \frac{C_n(q)}{|GL_n(q)|}x^n=\prod_{i\ge1}\prod_{j\ge0}(1-q^{1-j}x^i)^{-1}$ for commuting pairs.

## Distinct character degrees of the symmetric group | 5 | Algebra
W: Conjugate partitions always give representations of the same dimension, so at most about half of the p(n) irreducible representations of S_n can have distinct dimensions. Decide what fraction of p(n) the number of distinct dimensions really is in the limit — in particular whether it tends to one half, to zero, or to something in between.
M: Let $d(n)=\#\{f^\lambda:\lambda\vdash n\}$ be the number of distinct irreducible character degrees of $S_n$. Determine whether $\lim_{n\to\infty}d(n)/p(n)$ exists and find it; in particular decide whether $d(n)/p(n)\to 0$. (Computed by the hook length formula: $d(n)/p(n)$ lies in $[0.40,0.47]$ for $20\le n\le45$, e.g. $d(40)=16216$, $p(40)=37338$, ratio $0.434$; prime $n$ give smaller ratios, e.g. $0.405$ at $n=29$, $0.412$ at $n=41$.)

## The complex of planar graphs | 5 | Topology
W: Planar graphs on n labelled vertices form a simplicial complex: delete an edge and the graph stays planar. Every maximal planar graph has 3n − 6 edges, so the complex is pure. Show it is a wedge of top-dimensional spheres and count them.
M: Let $\Pi_n$ be the simplicial complex on vertex set $E(K_n)$ whose faces are edge sets of planar graphs. It is pure of dimension $3n-7$. Prove that $\Pi_n$ is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of $(3n-7)$-spheres (or shellable), and find the number $a_n$ of spheres. Exact computation of the reduced Euler characteristic from all graphs on at most 7 vertices gives $\Pi_4\simeq$ point, $\Pi_5=\partial\Delta^9\cong S^8$, $\tilde\chi(\Pi_6)=-15$, $\tilde\chi(\Pi_7)=177$, consistent with $a_5=1,a_6=15,a_7=177$. Find the growth rate of $a_n$ and the $S_n$-representation on $\tilde H_{3n-7}(\Pi_n)$.

## Hard kings at activity minus one | 5 | Topology
W: For the hard-square gas on a torus the weighted count at activity −1 stays small, because the transfer matrix at −1 has only roots of unity as eigenvalues. For non-attacking kings it grows exponentially instead. Find its exponential growth rate and the homotopy type behind it.
M: Let $G_{m,n}$ be the king's graph on the torus $\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n$ (cells adjacent if they differ by at most 1 in each coordinate mod $m,n$) and $\mathrm{Ind}(G_{m,n})$ its independence complex, with $\tilde\chi=-Z_{m,n}(-1)$ where $Z_{m,n}(z)=\sum_I z^{|I|}$. Transfer-matrix computation for $3\le m,n\le 12$ gives e.g. $\tilde\chi(\mathrm{Ind}(G_{8,8}))=20875$ and $\tilde\chi(\mathrm{Ind}(G_{10,10}))=2236323$, growing irregularly in sign and size. Prove that $\kappa=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1{n^2}\log|Z_{n,n}(-1)|$ is positive and determine it; determine the dimensions of the nonzero homology groups of $\mathrm{Ind}(G_{m,n})$, and decide whether it is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres.

## Permanental hypersurfaces are polynomial count | 6 | Algebra
W: Kontsevich asked whether graph hypersurfaces always have point counts that are polynomials in the field size; that failed. Ask the same of the hypersurface where the n × n permanent vanishes: does the number of zero-permanent matrices over a field with q elements (q odd) always come out as a polynomial in q?
M: For odd $q$ let $P_n(q)=\#\{X\in M_n(\mathbb F_q):\operatorname{per}X=0\}$. Prove or disprove that for every $n$ there is a polynomial $F_n\in\mathbb Z[x]$ with $P_n(q)=F_n(q)$ for all odd prime powers $q$ (or for all $q$ in some residue classes). Checked: $P_3(q)=q^8+q^2(q-1)(5q^3-6q^2+3q-1)$ for $q=3,5,7,11,13,17$ (via the count $5q^3-6q^2+3q-1$ of $2\times3$ matrices with all three $2\times2$ permanents zero); $P_4(3)=17116353$, $P_4(5)=32366450625$, $P_4(7)=4863715492993$.

## Alternating quotients of knot groups | 6 | Topology
W: The trefoil's group maps onto every large alternating group because it maps onto the modular group. Show that every nontrivial knot group maps onto the alternating group A_n for all large n, and bound the last exception in terms of the knot.
M: Prove that for every nontrivial knot $K\subset S^3$ there is $N(K)$ such that $\pi_1(S^3\setminus K)$ surjects onto $A_n$ for all $n\ge N(K)$, and bound $N(K)$ by a function of the crossing number. Exhaustive search over pairs of conjugate even permutations satisfying the Wirtinger-type relation $wx=yw$ shows: the trefoil ($w=xy$) surjects onto $A_5$ but not onto $A_6,A_7,A_8$ (and onto $A_n$ for $n\ge9$ via $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ and Miller's $(2,3)$-generation); the figure-eight knot ($w=x^{-1}yxy^{-1}$) surjects onto $A_6$, $A_9$, $A_{10}$ but not onto $A_5,A_7,A_8$. Is $N(4_1)=9$?

## Knotted Hamiltonian polygons | 6 | Topology
W: Put n points in space in general position and join them in a random order into a closed polygon. Every such point set with 7 points has some knotted Hamiltonian polygon. Decide whether, for every large point set, almost all of its Hamiltonian polygons are knotted.
M: For a set $X\subset\mathbb R^3$ of $n$ points in general position let $\kappa(X)$ be the fraction of the $(n-1)!/2$ Hamiltonian cycles of the rectilinear $K_n$ on $X$ that are nontrivial knots. Prove or disprove: $\min_{|X|=n}\kappa(X)\to1$ as $n\to\infty$. If false, determine $\liminf_n\min_{|X|=n}\kappa(X)$ and describe extremal configurations. Monte Carlo evidence, using knot determinant $\ne1$ as a certificate of knottedness for uniformly random Hamiltonian cycles: for points on the moment curve $(t,t^2,t^3)$ at equally spaced $t$ the certified fraction is $0.007,0.04,0.16,0.45,0.88$ for $n=8,12,16,24,40$; for points on a circle with heights $10^{-3}i$ it is $0.49,0.79,0.97$ for $n=16,24,40$; for uniform points in a cube $0.31,0.61,1.0$. Among these the moment curve is the least knotted; decide whether some configuration keeps a positive fraction of unknots for all $n$.

## Principally nonsingular matrices over finite fields | 4 | Algebra
W: Count the square matrices over a finite field all of whose principal minors are nonzero. Triangular matrices with nonzero diagonal show there are at least about q^(n²/2) of them, while each of the 2^n − 1 minors is a separate constraint. Find the true exponential order of the count.
M: Let $c_n(q)=\#\{A\in M_n(\mathbb F_q):\det A_{S,S}\ne0\ \forall\,\emptyset\ne S\subseteq[n]\}$. Over $\mathbb F_2$, $A\mapsto A+I$ identifies these with matrices all of whose principal submatrices are nilpotent, which are exactly the zero-diagonal matrices with acyclic support, so $c_n(2)$ is the number of labelled acyclic digraphs (checked: $1,3,25,543,29281$). For $q\ge3$ no such reduction exists: $c_2(q)=(q-1)^2(q^2-q+1)$, $c_3(3)=2392$, $c_3(5)=520000$ (exhaustive). Determine $\gamma(q)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1{n^2}\log_q c_n(q)$ for each $q\ge3$ (it lies in $[\tfrac12,1]$ if it exists), in particular decide whether $\gamma(q)=\tfrac12$, and find a recursion for $c_n(q)$.

## Subgroup growth of the figure-eight knot group | 7 | Topology
W: Count the ways the figure-eight knot group can act on n points. For the trefoil the answer grows like a known fractional power of n! times n!, coming from the modular group. Find the exact power for the figure-eight knot, a hyperbolic knot.
M: Let $h_n(K)=|\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_1(S^3\setminus K),S_n)|$. For the trefoil, $\pi_1\to\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)\cong C_2*C_3$ gives $h_n(3_1)\ge (n!)^{7/6+o(1)}$. Determine $\alpha(K)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\log\big(h_n(K)/n!\big)/\log n!$ for $K=4_1$ (prove the limit exists and compute it); more generally decide whether $\alpha(K)$ depends only on simple data of $K$ (for example its hyperbolic volume, or the largest $1/[\pi_1:H]$ over finite-index $H$ surjecting onto a free group of rank 2). Exhaustive counts with $\pi_1(S^3\setminus4_1)=\langle x,y\mid wx=yw\rangle$, $w=x^{-1}yxy^{-1}$: $h_n(4_1)=1,2,6,48,600,10080,110880$ for $n=1,\dots,7$ (trefoil: $1,2,12,96,600,6480,85680$).

## Diagrams in which every crossing unknots | 5 | Topology
W: In the usual pictures of the trefoil and the figure-eight knot, switching any single crossing gives the unknot. Show these are essentially the only reduced knot diagrams with this property.
M: Call a knot diagram $D$ totally unknotting if $D$ represents a nontrivial knot, $D$ is reduced (no nugatory crossings), and changing any one crossing of $D$ yields a diagram of the unknot. Prove that every totally unknotting diagram is, up to planar isotopy and mirror image, the standard 3-crossing diagram of $3_1$ or the standard 4-crossing diagram of $4_1$; or exhibit another one. (Necessary conditions: unknotting number 1 and $|\sigma(K)|\le2$; in a twist knot diagram, changing a twist crossing gives the unknot only when there are at most two half-twists.)

## Signature of a random positive 3-braid | 4 | Topology
W: Write a long random word in the two positive generators of the three-strand braid group and close it up into a knot or link. Its signature grows linearly with the length. Find the exact rate.
M: Let $\beta_L=\sigma_{i_1}\cdots\sigma_{i_L}\in B_3$ with $i_k\in\{1,2\}$ i.i.d. uniform, and $\hat\beta_L$ its closure. By the Gambaudo–Ghys theorem the signature is a quasimorphism on $B_3$, so $c=-\lim_{L\to\infty}\sigma(\hat\beta_L)/L$ exists almost surely. Determine $c$ in closed form (it lies in $(0,1]$; for the constant word $\sigma_1^L$ the rate is $1$), and prove a central limit theorem for $\sigma(\hat\beta_L)+cL$ with an explicit variance. Do the same for the Levine–Tristram signatures $\sigma_\omega$ as functions of $\omega\in S^1$.

## Sandpile group of the transposition graph | 5 | Algebra
W: Join two permutations when they differ by swapping two entries. The Laplacian eigenvalues of this graph are integers given by contents of Young diagrams, so the number of spanning trees is known, but the finer structure of its sandpile group is not. Determine that group.
M: Let $\Gamma_n=\mathrm{Cay}(S_n,\{\text{all transpositions}\})$ and $K(\Gamma_n)=\mathbb Z^{n!}_0/\Delta\mathbb Z^{n!}$ its critical (sandpile) group. Its order is $\frac1{n!}\prod_{\lambda\ne(n)}\big(\binom n2-c(\lambda)\big)^{(f^\lambda)^2}$ with $c(\lambda)$ the content sum. Determine the invariant factors of $K(\Gamma_n)$ for all $n$. Smith normal form computation of the reduced Laplacian gives $K(\Gamma_3)\cong\mathbb Z_3^2\oplus\mathbb Z_9$, $K(\Gamma_4)\cong\mathbb Z_2^4\oplus\mathbb Z_{16}^5\oplus\mathbb Z_{64}\oplus\mathbb Z_{192}^2\oplus\mathbb Z_{576}$, $K(\Gamma_5)\cong\mathbb Z_5^{14}\oplus\mathbb Z_{20}^{10}\oplus\mathbb Z_{80}\oplus\mathbb Z_{480}^5\oplus\mathbb Z_{2400}^3\oplus\mathbb Z_{14400}^{16}$. In particular decide whether the exponent of $K(\Gamma_n)$ equals $(n!)^2$ for all $n\ge4$, and find the multiplicity of each prime-power cyclic factor in terms of characters of $S_n$.

## The complex of non-Hamiltonian graphs | 5 | Topology
W: Graphs on n labelled vertices with no Hamiltonian cycle form a simplicial complex, since deleting edges cannot create a Hamiltonian cycle. Its Euler characteristic appears to be exactly −(n−1)!, the same count that governs the complex of disconnected graphs. Prove this and find the homotopy type.
M: Let $\mathrm{NH}_n$ be the simplicial complex on $E(K_n)$ whose faces are edge sets of graphs on $[n]$ with no Hamiltonian cycle. Exact computation over all graphs with at most 7 vertices gives $\tilde\chi(\mathrm{NH}_n)=-1,-2,-6,-24,-120$ for $n=3,\dots,7$. Prove $\tilde\chi(\mathrm{NH}_n)=-(n-1)!$ for all $n\ge3$, and determine the homotopy type of $\mathrm{NH}_n$; in particular decide whether it is a wedge of $(n-1)!$ spheres of one odd dimension and find that dimension (the complex itself has dimension $\binom{n-1}2$), and identify the $S_n$-representation on the reduced homology: is it $\mathrm{Lie}_n\otimes\mathrm{sgn}$, as for the complex of disconnected graphs, whose Euler characteristic is also $\pm(n-1)!$? (For comparison, the complex of graphs with no Hamiltonian path has $\tilde\chi=2,-2,-11,-54,-302$ for $n=3,\dots,7$.)

## Stick knots on the moment curve | 5 | Topology
W: Choose n points on the twisted cubic curve (t, t², t³) and join them by straight sticks in some order. Because all such point sets look alike combinatorially, the knot depends only on the order. Find how many points each knot needs, and compare with the ordinary stick number.
M: For a knot $K$ let $s_\gamma(K)$ be the least $n$ such that $K$ is the polygon $\gamma(t_{\sigma(1)})\gamma(t_{\sigma(2)})\cdots\gamma(t_{\sigma(n)})$ for some $t_1<\dots<t_n$ and $\sigma\in S_n$, where $\gamma(t)=(t,t^2,t^3)$ (the knot type depends only on $\sigma$). Finiteness follows from Negami's Ramsey theorem. Prove $s_\gamma(K)\le C\,s(K)$ for the stick number $s(K)$ and an absolute $C$, or show $s_\gamma(K)/s(K)$ is unbounded; find $s_\gamma(T_{2,2k+1})$ asymptotically. Exhaustive check of all cyclic orders (knot determinant as invariant): for $n=6$ every polygon has determinant 1, so $s_\gamma(3_1)=7>6=s(3_1)$; for $n=7$ exactly one of 360 cycles is a trefoil; determinant-5 knots ($4_1$ or $5_1$) first appear at $n=9$ (10 of 20160 cycles), so $s_\gamma(4_1)\ge9>7=s(4_1)$. Since every rectilinear $K_n$ on moment-curve points must contain $K$ in order for Negami's number $R(K)$ (least $n$ with every rectilinear $K_n$ containing $K$ as a cycle) to be $\le n$, $R(K)\ge s_\gamma(K)$; decide whether $R(K)$ is bounded by a polynomial in the crossing number.

## Independence complexes of hypercubes | 5 | Topology
W: Take the n-dimensional cube graph and form the simplicial complex of its independent vertex sets. Its Euler characteristic stays tiny for small n and then jumps. Determine the homotopy type of this complex for every n.
M: Let $I_n=\mathrm{Ind}(Q_n)$ be the independence complex of the hypercube graph $Q_n$ on $\{0,1\}^n$. Exact computation (recursive and transfer-matrix over $Q_6=Q_3\square Q_3$) gives $\tilde\chi(I_n)=1,1,-3,-7,-11,-143$ for $n=1,\dots,6$. Determine the homotopy type of $I_n$ for all $n$: decide whether it is a wedge of spheres, find the dimensions that occur (by bipartiteness, $-\tilde\chi(I_n)=\sum_{S\subseteq E,\,N(S)=O}(-1)^{|S|}$ where $E,O$ are the even and odd vertices) and the growth rate of $|\tilde\chi(I_n)|$; in particular decide whether $\log|\tilde\chi(I_n)|$ grows like $c\,2^n$ or more slowly.
