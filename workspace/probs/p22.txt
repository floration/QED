## Kneser graph of Hamiltonian cycles | 5 | Combinatorics
W: Make a graph whose vertices are the Hamiltonian cycles of the complete graph on n vertices, joining two cycles when they share no edge. Simple colourings use n − 3 colours; show you can never do better, even though fractional and clique bounds only give about n/2.
M: For $n\ge5$ let $\mathcal H_n$ be the graph on the Hamiltonian cycles of $K_n$ (as edge sets), two cycles adjacent iff they are edge-disjoint. Colouring each cycle by an edge $\{1,j\}$, $2\le j\le n-4$, that it contains, and putting all remaining cycles (whose two edges at vertex $1$ both go into $\{n-2,n-1,n\}$, hence pairwise intersect) in one extra class, gives $\chi(\mathcal H_n)\le n-3$, while vertex-transitivity and Walecki's decomposition give only $\chi_f(\mathcal H_n)=\frac{n-1}{2}$ for odd $n$. Prove that $\chi(\mathcal H_n)=n-3$ for all $n\ge5$. (Verified by SAT for $5\le n\le 8$; note $n-3$ is one less than the general bound $|E(K_n)|-\mathrm{ex}(K_n,C_n)=n-2$.)

## Kneser graph of non-crossing matchings | 4 | Combinatorics
W: Pair up 2m points on a circle with non-crossing chords in all possible ways, and join two pairings when they share no chord. Colouring by the partner of point 1 uses m colours; prove that fewer is impossible, although the fractional bound stays below 4.
M: Let $\mathcal M_m$ be the set of non-crossing perfect matchings of $2m$ points in convex position ($|\mathcal M_m|=C_m$), and let $KG(\mathcal M_m)$ join two matchings iff they have no common edge. Colouring by the partner of point $1$ shows $\chi(KG(\mathcal M_m))\le m$, while the matchings containing a fixed boundary edge give $\alpha\ge C_{m-1}$, so the trivial lower bounds are $O(1)$. Prove $\chi(KG(\mathcal M_m))=m$ for all $m\ge2$ (verified by SAT for $m\le7$), and determine $\alpha(KG(\mathcal M_m))$ (is it $C_{m-1}$, attained only by boundary-edge stars?). This is the matching analogue of the theorem that the Kneser graph of triangulations of a convex $n$-gon has chromatic number $n-2$.

## Dimension of the dominance lattice | 4 | Combinatorics
W: Partitions of n are ordered by dominance: one is below another if each of its running totals of largest parts is no bigger. How many linear orders must be intersected to recreate this order? Running totals show at most n − 1 are needed; find the true growth rate.
M: Let $L_n$ be the set of partitions of $n$ under dominance, $\lambda\trianglelefteq\mu$ iff $\sum_{i\le k}\lambda_i\le\sum_{i\le k}\mu_i$ for all $k$. The map $\lambda\mapsto(\lambda_1,\lambda_1+\lambda_2,\dots)$ embeds $L_n$ into a product of $n-1$ chains, so $\dim L_n\le n-1$. Independent one-box moves from row $i$ to row $i+1$ in $(2k,2k-2,\dots,2)$ give a copy of the Boolean lattice $B_{k-1}$ with $n=k(k+1)$, so $\dim L_n\ge c\sqrt n$. Determine the order of growth of $\dim L_n$: is $\dim L_n=\Theta(\sqrt n)$, $\Theta(n)$, or strictly in between? In particular, is $\dim L_n=o(n)$?

## Linear grid minors in random cubic graphs | 5 | Combinatorics
W: Does a random 3-regular graph on n vertices contain, as a minor, a square grid using a constant fraction of all n vertices? Expander methods only give grids of side about the square root of n over log n.
M: Let $G_n$ be a uniformly random cubic graph on $n$ vertices. Is there a constant $c>0$ such that, with high probability, $G_n$ contains the $k\times k$ grid as a minor for $k=\lfloor c\sqrt n\rfloor$? More generally, does $G_n$ whp contain every planar graph of maximum degree $3$ on $cn$ vertices as a minor? Known minor-universality results for expanders give only $k=\Theta(\sqrt{n/\log n})$, and the $\log n$ loss is unavoidable for universality over all bounded-degree graphs, so a positive answer must exploit planarity.

## Turán exponent of the Pasch configuration | 6 | Combinatorics
W: The Pasch configuration is four triples on six points, like four alternate faces of an octahedron. How many triples can a system on n points have while avoiding it? A Sidon-set construction gives about n^2.5 triples and the octahedron bound gives about n^2.75; find the true exponent.
M: Let $P=\{abc,ade,bdf,cef\}$ and $\mathrm{ex}_3(n,P)$ the maximum number of edges of an $n$-vertex $3$-graph with no copy of $P$ (not necessarily linear). Every copy of $P$ in a $3$-partite $3$-graph uses two vertices per part in the pattern $(a_1b_1c_1),(a_1b_2c_2),(a_2b_1c_2),(a_2b_2c_1)$; hence taking all $(a,b,c)\in[n]^3$ with $c-b\in S$, $S$ a Sidon set, gives $\mathrm{ex}_3(n,P)=\Omega(n^{5/2})$, while $P\subset K^{(3)}_{2,2,2}$ gives $O(n^{11/4})$. Determine $\lim \log\mathrm{ex}_3(n,P)/\log n$ if it exists; in particular decide whether $\mathrm{ex}_3(n,P)=n^{5/2+o(1)}$.

## Few symbols in a Latin subarray | 6 | Combinatorics
W: In any large Latin square, a greedy choice of k rows and k columns produces a k-by-k block using at most k² − 2k + 3 different symbols. Can one always do one better? This would be a grid-shaped case of the Brown–Erdős–Sós phenomenon, and random Latin squares suggest the true answer is within one or two of this.
M: For $k\ge3$ let $g_k$ be the least $m$ such that every Latin square of order $n\ge n_0(k)$ has $k$ rows $R$ and $k$ columns $C$ with $|L(R\times C)|\le m$. Choosing rows $r_1,r_2$ and columns $c_1,\dots,c_k$ with $L(r_2,c_i)=L(r_1,c_{i+1})$, then adding rows one at a time each repeating an existing symbol, shows $g_k\le k^2-2k+3$; the heuristic count $n^{2k+m-k^2}$ of $k\times k$ subarrays with $m$ symbols in a random Latin square suggests $g_k\ge k^2-2k$. Prove $g_k\le k^2-2k+2$ for all $k\ge3$ (for $k=2$ the analogous statement fails because intercalate-free squares exist), and determine $g_k$ exactly.

## Cauchy–Davenport Latin squares | 5 | Combinatorics
W: In the addition table of the integers mod a prime p, any a rows and b columns meet in at least a + b − 1 different symbols (the Cauchy–Davenport theorem). Show that no other Latin square has this property: it should characterise the prime cyclic groups among all Latin squares.
M: Say a Latin square $L$ of order $n$ is CD if $|L(A\times B)|\ge\min(n,|A|+|B|-1)$ for all nonempty row sets $A$ and column sets $B$. Prove that $L$ is CD if and only if $n$ is prime and $L$ is isotopic to the Cayley table of $\mathbb Z_n$. Note that CD forces every pair of rows (and of columns) to induce an $n$-cycle, so CD squares are row- and column-Hamiltonian; non-group squares with this weaker property exist (e.g. of order $11$), so the full CD condition must be used. Verified exhaustively for $n\le7$: for $n=7$ the only reduced Latin squares with no $2\times2$ or $2\times3$ (or $3\times2$) subarray closed under the row (column) permutation are the $120$ reduced isotopes of $\mathbb Z_7$; for $n=6$ there are none.

## How many permutations have no three in line? | 5 | Combinatorics
W: Count the permutations of 1 to n whose graph, drawn as n points in the grid, has no three points on a common straight line. The count seems to behave like n! raised to a fixed power near 0.8. Prove that a fixed exponent strictly between 0 and 1 exists and find it.
M: Let $a(n)$ be the number of $\sigma\in S_n$ such that no three of the points $(i,\sigma(i))$ are collinear (OEIS A179040). Prove that $\theta=\lim_{n\to\infty}\log a(n)/\log n!$ exists and that $0<\theta<1$, and determine $\theta$. Simulations (n up to 400) suggest a random permutation has on the order of $n\log n$ collinear triples, which via a Poisson heuristic suggests $a(n)=(n!)^{1-c'+o(1)}$; exact counts for $8\le n\le12$ (computed by backtracking) give $\log a(n)/\log n!\in[0.807,0.822]$. Even $\liminf\log a(n)/\log n!>0$ (exponentially-in-$n\log n$ many such permutations) and $\limsup<1$ are open as far as known.

## Product-free sets in quasigroups | 5 | Combinatorics
W: Every group of order n has a product-free subset of size at least about n^(11/14), but a general Latin square (quasigroup) need not behave like a group. Show the right universal answer for quasigroups is of order the square root of n log n, attained by random Latin squares.
M: For a quasigroup $(Q,\circ)$ of order $n$ let $\mathrm{pf}(Q)$ be the largest $S\subseteq Q$ with no $x,y,z\in S$ (not necessarily distinct) satisfying $x\circ y=z$. The triples $\{x,y,x\circ y\}$ form a $3$-graph with $n^2$ edges and bounded codegrees, so the Ajtai–Komlós–Pintz–Spencer–Szemerédi bound gives $\mathrm{pf}(Q)\ge c\sqrt{n\log n}$ for every $Q$. Prove that this is sharp: there are quasigroups (presumably almost all Latin squares) with $\mathrm{pf}(Q)\le C\sqrt{n\log n}$. More precisely, determine $\lim \mathrm{pf}(Q_n)/\sqrt{n\log n}$ for a uniformly random Latin square $Q_n$, in probability.

## Typical flip distance | 6 | Combinatorics
W: Two triangulations of a convex n-gon are chosen independently at random. How many diagonal flips does it take to turn one into the other? The worst case is known to be 2n − 10; find the typical value, which lies somewhere between about 0.9n and 2n.
M: Let $T_n,T_n'$ be independent uniform triangulations of a convex $n$-gon and $d_n$ their flip distance (equivalently the rotation distance of two uniform binary trees). Prove that $d_n/n\to c$ in probability for some constant $c$, and determine $c$. Trivially $d_n\ge n-3-|T_n\cap T_n'|$ and $\mathbb E|T_n\cap T_n'|\sim n\sum_{m\ge1}(C_m4^{-m})^2\approx0.093\,n$, while $d_n\le 2n-10$. Is $c>1-\sum_{m\ge1}(C_m4^{-m})^2$, i.e. do typical pairs require a positive fraction of "wasted" flips? Is $c<2$?

## Discrepancy of triangulations | 5 | Combinatorics
W: Colour every diagonal of a convex n-gon red or blue. Can this be done so that every one of the Catalan-many triangulations uses nearly equal numbers of red and blue diagonals? Small cases show the unavoidable imbalance grows, but very slowly; decide whether it stays bounded, grows like log n, or grows like a power of n.
M: For a $2$-colouring $\chi$ of the diagonals of a convex $n$-gon let $\mathrm{disc}(\chi)=\max_T|\sum_{d\in T}\chi(d)|$ over all triangulations $T$ (with $\chi=\pm1$), and $D(n)=\min_\chi\mathrm{disc}(\chi)$. Exhaustive search and SAT give $D(5),\dots,D(12)=2,1,2,3,2,3,4,3$. Determine the order of growth of $D(n)$: is $D(n)\to\infty$, and if so is $D(n)=\Theta(\log n)$, $\Theta(\sqrt n)$, or something else? Note that a colouring by parity of $i+j$ for the diagonal $\{i,j\}$ already fails badly (some triangulation uses almost only even diagonals), so $D(n)=o(n)$ is itself not obvious.

## Polychromatic colourings of triangulations | 4 | Combinatorics
W: Colour the diagonals of a convex n-gon with as many colours as possible so that every triangulation contains a diagonal of every colour. Small cases suggest the maximum is exactly the integer part of n/3, while simple counting only rules out more than about n/2 colours.
M: Let $p(n)$ be the largest $r$ for which the diagonals of a convex $n$-gon can be partitioned into $r$ classes each meeting every triangulation. Each class is a blocking set, and the minimum blocking set has $n-2$ diagonals (e.g. all ear diagonals but two adjacent ones), so $p(n)\le\frac{n(n-3)}{2(n-2)}$. SAT computation gives $p(n)=\lfloor n/3\rfloor$ for $5\le n\le10$, and $p(11)\ge3$, $p(12)\ge4$. Prove $p(n)=\lfloor n/3\rfloor$ for all $n\ge5$, or at least determine $\lim p(n)/n$.

## Monochromatic triangulated polygons | 5 | Combinatorics
W: Colour all sides and diagonals of a convex n-gon red and blue. How large a convex k-gon on some of the vertices can one always find whose boundary and some triangulation of it are all one colour (any triangulation allowed)? The answer is somewhere between about n^(1/7) and the square root of n; find the exponent.
M: Let $f(n)$ be the largest $k$ such that in every $2$-colouring of the edges of the convex complete graph $K_n$ there are vertices $v_1<\dots<v_k$ (in cyclic order) and a triangulation of the convex polygon $v_1\cdots v_k$ whose $2k-3$ edges (sides $v_iv_{i+1}$, $v_kv_1$ and the $k-3$ diagonals) are monochromatic. The ordered Ramsey number of the monotone cycle, $2k^2-6k+6$, gives $f(n)=O(\sqrt n)$, and the polynomial convex Ramsey bound $20k^7$ for pathwidth-$2$ outerplanar triangulations (e.g. zigzag strips) gives $f(n)=\Omega(n^{1/7})$. Determine $\lim\log f(n)/\log n$; in particular, is $f(n)=n^{1/2-o(1)}$?

## Dominating the associahedron | 5 | Combinatorics
W: Choose a set of triangulations of a convex n-gon so that every triangulation is either chosen or one diagonal flip away from a chosen one. Each choice covers only n − 2 triangulations, so at least a 1/(n−2) fraction must be chosen. Is that nearly enough, as for error-correcting codes in the hypercube?
M: Let $F_n$ be the flip graph of triangulations of a convex $n$-gon ($C_{n-2}$ vertices, $(n-3)$-regular) and $\gamma_n$ its domination number, so $\gamma_n\ge C_{n-2}/(n-2)$. Computation gives $\gamma_5,\gamma_6,\gamma_7=2,4,10$, and $F_8$ has no perfect code even though $6\mid C_6=132$. Is $\gamma_n\sim C_{n-2}/(n-2)$ (asymptotically perfect domination, as $\gamma(Q_n)\sim2^n/n$)? If not, determine $\lim \gamma_n(n-2)/C_{n-2}$, or at least show it is finite.

## Colouring the matching switch graph | 5 | Combinatorics
W: Take all perfect matchings of 2n points, and join two matchings when one is turned into the other by swapping partners within two pairs. The smallest cases need exactly n + 1 colours. Is that the answer for every n? The best general colouring known to me uses about 2n colours.
M: Let $\mathcal P_n$ be the graph on the $(2n-1)!!$ perfect matchings of $K_{2n}$, with $M\sim M'$ iff $M\triangle M'$ is a $4$-cycle. Labelling the vertices by distinct elements $x_v$ of $\mathbb F_q$ ($q\ge2n$ prime) and colouring $M$ by $\sum_{ab\in M}x_ax_b$ is proper, since a switch $\{ab,cd\}\to\{ac,bd\}$ changes the sum by $(x_a-x_d)(x_c-x_b)\neq0$; hence $\chi(\mathcal P_n)\le q$. SAT computation gives $\chi(\mathcal P_n)=n+1$ for $n=2,3,4$, while $\omega(\mathcal P_n)=3$. Prove $\chi(\mathcal P_n)=n+1$ for all $n$, or determine $\lim\chi(\mathcal P_n)/n$ (is it $1$ or $2$?).

## Red triangulations in random colourings | 6 | Probability
W: Colour each diagonal of a convex n-gon red independently with probability p. When is there a triangulation using only red diagonals? Simulations show a sharp change near p = 0.4: above it a fully red triangulation exists, below it even the best triangulation misses a fixed fraction of red. Find the critical value and prove the transition is sharp.
M: Let each diagonal of a convex $n$-gon be red independently with probability $p$, and let $R_n(p)$ be the maximum number of red diagonals in a triangulation (computable by an $O(n^3)$ dynamic program). Prove that $c(p)=\lim R_n(p)/(n-3)$ exists a.s., that there is $p_c\in(0,1)$ with $c(p)<1$ for $p<p_c$ and $c(p)=1$ for $p>p_c$, and determine $p_c$; also decide whether $\Pr[R_n(p)=n-3]\to1$ for every $p>p_c$. Simulations (DP, $n\le2500$) give $c(0.30)\approx0.94$, $c(0.37)\lesssim0.99$ and decreasing in $n$, while for $p\ge0.42$ every sample had $R_n=n-3$, suggesting $p_c\approx0.40$.

## Order dimension of the partition lattice | 5 | Combinatorics
W: The set partitions of an n-element set, ordered by refinement, form a lattice. How many linear orders must be intersected to reproduce it? Simple arguments give at least n − 1 and at most n(n−1)/2; decide whether the answer is linear, quadratic, or in between.
M: Let $\Pi_n$ be the partition lattice of $[n]$. The atoms $\{i,n\}$ and coatoms $\{\{j\},[n]\setminus\{j\}\}$, $1\le i,j\le n-1$, form a standard example $S_{n-1}$, so $\dim\Pi_n\ge n-1$; since the join-irreducibles of $\Pi_n$ are its $\binom n2$ atoms, $\dim\Pi_n\le\binom n2$. Determine the order of growth of $\dim\Pi_n$. In particular, is $\dim\Pi_n=O(n)$, or is $\dim\Pi_n/n\to\infty$?
