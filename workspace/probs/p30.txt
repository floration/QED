## Subtracting norms | 4 | Games
W: In the game where players alternately remove from a pile a positive number of stones that is a sum of two squares (or, more generally, a value of x² + xy + y², x² + 2y², x² + xy + 2y², x² + xy + 3y² or x² + xy + 5y²), the losing pile sizes, computed up to 100000, form an exact digit pattern: q times the numbers whose base-q² digits are all below q, where q is the smallest prime that cannot be split in that number ring. Prove this pattern holds for every pile size.
M: Let $f$ be the principal form of discriminant $D\in\{-3,-4,-7,-8,-11,-19\}$ and $S_f=\{f(x,y)>0: x,y\in\mathbb Z\}$. In the subtraction game with move set $S_f$, let $q$ be the least prime inert in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$ ($q=2,3,3,5,2,2$ respectively). Prove that the set of $\mathcal P$-positions equals $\{\,q\sum_i d_i q^{2i} : 0\le d_i\le q-1\,\}$. (Verified for all pile sizes $\le 10^5$ for each $D$, and $\le 2\cdot 10^6$ for $D=-4$; the analogous statement fails for $D=-43,-67,-163$.)

## Losing piles for Euler's form | 6 | Games
W: Remove from a pile any positive value of Euler's prime-generating form x² + xy + 41y². Unlike the sums-of-two-squares game, the losing pile sizes show no digit pattern; there are 16 up to 100, 164 up to 100000 and 376 up to a million. Show that the number of losing pile sizes up to N grows at least like a fixed positive power of N, and determine the exponent.
M: Let $S=\{x^2+xy+41y^2>0: x,y\in\mathbb Z\}$ and let $\mathcal P\subset\mathbb N$ be the $\mathcal P$-positions of the subtraction game with move set $S$. Prove there is $c>0$ with $|\mathcal P\cap[0,N]|\ge N^{c}$ for large $N$, and decide whether $\lim_{N\to\infty}\log|\mathcal P\cap[0,N]|/\log N$ exists (computed counts: $16,33,70,164,376$ at $N=10^2,\dots,10^6$). Trivially $|\mathcal P\cap[0,N]|\gg N/|S\cap[0,N]|\asymp\sqrt{\log N}$, and Sárközy-type bounds give $o(N)$.
## Odd losses in Fibonacci subtraction | 6 | Games
W: Players alternately remove a Fibonacci number of stones from a pile; whoever cannot move loses. About one sixth of pile sizes are losing, and nearly all of them are even: below 2^26 there are about 11.9 million losing sizes but only 27202 odd ones, the first being 169. Prove that odd losing sizes have density zero among all losing sizes, and decide whether the density of losing sizes converges.
M: Let $F=\{1,2,3,5,8,\dots\}$ and $\mathcal P$ the $\mathcal P$-positions of the subtraction game with move set $F$. Computation to $2^{26}$ gives $|\mathcal P\cap[0,x]|/x\in[0.163,0.178]$ oscillating with $\log_2 x$, and $|\mathcal P_{\rm odd}\cap[0,2^{26}]|=27202$ with $\min\mathcal P_{\rm odd}=169$. Prove $|\mathcal P_{\rm odd}\cap[0,x]|=o(|\mathcal P\cap[0,x]|)$, and determine whether $\lim_{x\to\infty}|\mathcal P\cap[0,x]|/x$ exists.
## Floor-quotient subtraction | 5 | Games
W: From a pile of n stones a player may remove ⌊n/k⌋ stones for any k ≥ 2 (when that is at least 1). The losing pile sizes begin 0, 1, 3, 7, 11, 15, 23, 31, 39, 47, 55, 63, 74, 85, 96, 114, and up to two million the i-th one is close to i²/4, with the error shrinking like a square root. Prove that the i-th losing pile size is asymptotic to i²/4.
M: In the game with moves $n\to n-\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ ($k\ge2$, $\lfloor n/k\rfloor\ge1$), let $p_0<p_1<\cdots$ be the $\mathcal P$-positions. Prove $p_i\sim i^2/4$. (Computed for $n\le 2\cdot10^6$: $p_i/i^2=0.3009,0.2858,0.2756$ at $i=512,1024,2048$, with $p_i/i^2-\tfrac14$ decaying roughly like $i^{-1/2}$.) Equivalently, $|\mathcal P\cap[0,x]|\sim 2\sqrt x$, matching the number of distinct moves available from $x$.
