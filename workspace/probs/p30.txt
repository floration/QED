## Subtracting norms | 4 | Games
W: In the game where players alternately remove from a pile a positive number of stones that is a sum of two squares (or, more generally, a value of x² + xy + y², x² + 2y², x² + xy + 2y², x² + xy + 3y² or x² + xy + 5y²), the losing pile sizes, computed up to 100000, form an exact digit pattern: q times the numbers whose base-q² digits are all below q, where q is the smallest prime that cannot be split in that number ring. Prove this pattern holds for every pile size.
M: Let $f$ be the principal form of discriminant $D\in\{-3,-4,-7,-8,-11,-19\}$ and $S_f=\{f(x,y)>0: x,y\in\mathbb Z\}$. In the subtraction game with move set $S_f$, let $q$ be the least prime inert in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$ ($q=2,3,3,5,2,2$ respectively). Prove that the set of $\mathcal P$-positions equals $\{\,q\sum_i d_i q^{2i} : 0\le d_i\le q-1\,\}$. (Verified for all pile sizes $\le 10^5$ for each $D$, and $\le 2\cdot 10^6$ for $D=-4$; the analogous statement fails for $D=-43,-67,-163$.)

## Losing piles for Euler's form | 6 | Games
W: Remove from a pile any positive value of Euler's prime-generating form x² + xy + 41y². Unlike the sums-of-two-squares game, the losing pile sizes show no digit pattern; there are 16 up to 100, 164 up to 100000 and 376 up to a million. Show that the number of losing pile sizes up to N grows at least like a fixed positive power of N, and determine the exponent.
M: Let $S=\{x^2+xy+41y^2>0: x,y\in\mathbb Z\}$ and let $\mathcal P\subset\mathbb N$ be the $\mathcal P$-positions of the subtraction game with move set $S$. Prove there is $c>0$ with $|\mathcal P\cap[0,N]|\ge N^{c}$ for large $N$, and decide whether $\lim_{N\to\infty}\log|\mathcal P\cap[0,N]|/\log N$ exists (computed counts: $16,33,70,164,376$ at $N=10^2,\dots,10^6$). Trivially $|\mathcal P\cap[0,N]|\gg N/|S\cap[0,N]|\asymp\sqrt{\log N}$, and Sárközy-type bounds give $o(N)$.
## Odd losses in Fibonacci subtraction | 6 | Games
W: Players alternately remove a Fibonacci number of stones from a pile; whoever cannot move loses. About one sixth of pile sizes are losing, and nearly all of them are even: below 2^26 there are about 11.9 million losing sizes but only 27202 odd ones, the first being 169. Prove that odd losing sizes have density zero among all losing sizes, and decide whether the density of losing sizes converges.
M: Let $F=\{1,2,3,5,8,\dots\}$ and $\mathcal P$ the $\mathcal P$-positions of the subtraction game with move set $F$. Computation to $2^{26}$ gives $|\mathcal P\cap[0,x]|/x\in[0.163,0.178]$ oscillating with $\log_2 x$, and $|\mathcal P_{\rm odd}\cap[0,2^{26}]|=27202$ with $\min\mathcal P_{\rm odd}=169$. Prove $|\mathcal P_{\rm odd}\cap[0,x]|=o(|\mathcal P\cap[0,x]|)$, and determine whether $\lim_{x\to\infty}|\mathcal P\cap[0,x]|/x$ exists.
## Floor-quotient subtraction | 5 | Games
W: From a pile of n stones a player may remove ⌊n/k⌋ stones for any k ≥ 2 (when that is at least 1). The losing pile sizes begin 0, 1, 3, 7, 11, 15, 23, 31, 39, 47, 55, 63, 74, 85, 96, 114, and up to two million the i-th one is close to i²/4, with the error shrinking like a square root. Prove that the i-th losing pile size is asymptotic to i²/4.
M: In the game with moves $n\to n-\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ ($k\ge2$, $\lfloor n/k\rfloor\ge1$), let $p_0<p_1<\cdots$ be the $\mathcal P$-positions. Prove $p_i\sim i^2/4$. (Computed for $n\le 2\cdot10^6$: $p_i/i^2=0.3009,0.2858,0.2756$ at $i=512,1024,2048$, with $p_i/i^2-\tfrac14$ decaying roughly like $i^{-1/2}$.) Equivalently, $|\mathcal P\cap[0,x]|\sim 2\sqrt x$, matching the number of distinct moves available from $x$.
## Tropical third-order recurrence | 6 | Dynamics
W: Start with any three integers and repeatedly append the larger of the last two minus the one before them. Every one of 1000 random starts tested, with entries up to 100000, returned to its start, with periods from 8 up to about 20 times the size of the entries. Prove every integer start is periodic, and explain why the periods grow linearly with size.
M: For $(x_0,x_1,x_2)\in\mathbb Z^3$ let $x_{n+3}=\max(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})-x_n$. Prove that every integer orbit is periodic. Further, show the minimal period satisfies $p(x)=O(\|x\|_\infty)$. (Tested: 200 random starts in each box $[-R,R]^3$, $R=10,\dots,10^5$, all periodic; maximal observed period $236,\,2422,\,20626,\,201662,\,2184186$; the orbit never exceeded about twice its initial size.) The map is piecewise linear, homogeneous and volume-preserving, so this is a lattice analogue of an area-preserving map of the projective plane.

## Square-root escape in an absolute-difference recurrence | 6 | Dynamics
W: Append to a sequence the absolute difference of its last two terms minus the term before them. Some starts cycle, but from −10, 5, 9 the terms grow without settling, the largest term after t steps being almost exactly 4√t (130, 404, 1285, 4057, 12845 after 10³ to 10⁷ steps). Prove that this orbit is unbounded with growth exactly of order √t, and identify the constant.
M: Let $x_{n+3}=|x_{n+2}-x_{n+1}|-x_n$ on $\mathbb Z$. For $(x_0,x_1,x_2)=(-10,5,9)$ prove $M_t:=\max_{n\le t}|x_n|$ satisfies $M_t\asymp\sqrt t$ and determine $\lim M_t/\sqrt t$ (numerically $\approx4.06$ for $t=10^3,\dots,10^7$). More generally, characterise the integer triples with periodic orbits: among 100 random starts in $[-R,R]^3$ ($R=10,100,1000$), roughly 70–76 did not return within $10^8$ steps while the rest were periodic with periods up to $7.3\cdot10^6$.
## The multiples game | 7 | Games
W: The numbers 2 to N are on a board. Players alternately pick a remaining number and erase it together with all its remaining multiples; whoever erases the last number wins. Exhaustive search for N up to 39 shows the second player wins only for N = 3, 7, 11, 23 and 32. Decide whether the second player wins for infinitely many N, and find any rule describing these N.
M: Let $G_N$ be the impartial game on $\{2,\dots,N\}$ in which a move chooses a remaining $x$ and deletes $\{kx:k\ge1\}$ from the remaining set (normal play). By exhaustive search, $G_N\in\mathcal P$ for $N\in\{3,7,11,23,32\}$ and $G_N\in\mathcal N$ for all other $2\le N\le39$. Prove or disprove: $\{N: G_N\in\mathcal P\}$ is infinite. (Unlike the divisor game Chomp, no strategy-stealing argument is available here.)
## The coprime game | 6 | Games
W: Players alternately name numbers from 2 to N, each new number coprime to every number named so far; whoever cannot move loses. Exhaustive search shows the second player wins exactly for N = 3, 4, 17, 18 and 23 to 28, and the first player wins for every other N up to 130. Prove the first player wins for every N ≥ 29.
M: Let $C_N$ be the game in which players alternately choose $x\in\{2,\dots,N\}$ coprime to all previously chosen numbers (normal play); equivalently, Node Kayles on the graph on $\{2,\dots,N\}$ joining numbers with a common prime factor. Computation gives $C_N\in\mathcal P$ iff $N\in\{3,4,17,18,23,24,\dots,28\}$ for $2\le N\le130$. Prove $C_N\in\mathcal N$ for all $N\ge29$. Note each prime $p\in(N/2,N]$ is an isolated single move, so the parity of $\pi(N)-\pi(N/2)$ enters, and a proof must control the Grundy value of the remaining core, not just its outcome.
## Square deletion | 6 | Games
W: A binary word is on the table; a move deletes any factor of the form ww (a block immediately repeated) and closes the gap; whoever cannot move loses. Among all binary words of length n the share of losing words, computed exactly, falls from 0.30 at n = 8 to 0.25 at n = 14. Decide whether this share tends to a positive limit or to zero.
M: For $w\in\{0,1\}^*$ let a move be $xuuy\to xy$ with $u\neq\varepsilon$ (normal play), and let $p_n=|\{w\in\{0,1\}^n: w\in\mathcal P\}|/2^n$. Exhaustively, $2^np_n=2,2,2,4,14,28,46,76,150,296,554,1100,2134,4106$ for $n=1,\dots,14$. Determine whether $\lim p_n$ exists and whether it is positive; also decide whether the $\mathcal P$-positions form a context-free language. (Every binary word of length $\ge4$ contains a square, so the only terminal positions have length $\le3$; the Grundy value of $0^n$ is $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, and $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ was the maximum Grundy value observed for $n\le14$.)
## Tropical wave equation on a ring | 6 | Dynamics
W: Put integers on a ring of L cells at two consecutive times, and get the next time by giving each cell the larger of its two neighbours' current values minus its own previous value. On 4 cells every one of 1000 random starts repeats with period exactly 12, but on 3 cells some starts repeat while others run off to infinity at a steady linear rate. Determine which starts on a 3-cell ring are periodic, and show the rest escape linearly.
M: On $\mathbb Z/L$ let $x_{t+1}(i)=\max(x_t(i-1),x_t(i+1))-x_{t-1}(i)$, the tropicalisation of the discrete wave equation. Tested: for $L=4$, all 1000 random initial data in $[-10^5,10^5]^8$ have period $12$; for $L=3$, out of 100 random starts in $[-R,R]^6$, $9$ ($R=10$) and $19$ ($R=100$) were not periodic within $10^8$ steps, while periodic ones had periods up to $3.5\cdot10^6$; non-periodic examples such as $x_0=(7,-6,-1)$, $x_1=(3,-6,7)$ have $\max_i|x_t(i)|$ growing linearly ($\approx0.027t$). For $L=3$, prove every integer orbit is either periodic or satisfies $\liminf_t \max_i|x_t(i)|/t>0$, and describe the set of periodic initial data. Prove also that period 12 is universal for $L=4$.
## Knight's Lights Out | 5 | Combinatorics
W: On an n × n board of lamps, pressing a lamp toggles it and every lamp a knight's move away. For some n every pattern can be switched off (n = 1–5, 10, 11, 18–23, 30–32, 36–41, 45–50, ...), for others not, and the number of independent unsolvable directions jumps around (it reaches 48 at n = 70). Find a rule for which n make every pattern solvable, and show the defect is unbounded.
M: Let $A_n$ be the $n^2\times n^2$ matrix over $\mathbb F_2$ of $I$ plus the knight-move adjacency of the $n\times n$ grid, and $d_n=\dim\ker A_n$. Computed: $d_n=0$ for $n\in\{1,\dots,5,10,11,18,\dots,23,30,31,32,36,\dots,41,45,\dots,50,54,55,56,65,\dots,68,75,76,77,81,85,86,90,\dots,95\}$ and $d_n>0$ for all other $n\le95$, with $\max_{n\le95}d_n=d_{70}=48$. Give a criterion (e.g. in terms of polynomials over $\mathbb F_2$, as for the classical $\sigma^+$ game) for $d_n=0$; prove that both $\{n:d_n=0\}$ and $\{n:d_n>0\}$ are infinite and that $\sup_n d_n=\infty$.
