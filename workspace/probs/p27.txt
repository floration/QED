## Do two permutations commute? | 4 | Computation
W: Alice holds one permutation of n items and Bob holds another. How many bits must they exchange, using shared randomness and allowing a small error, to decide whether the two permutations commute? A simple reduction from set disjointness shows about n bits are needed, and sending a whole permutation costs about n log n bits. Find the right answer.
M: For $\pi,\sigma\in S_n$ let $\mathrm{COMM}_n(\pi,\sigma)=1$ iff $\pi\sigma=\sigma\pi$, with $\pi$ given to Alice and $\sigma$ to Bob, and let $R(\mathrm{COMM}_n)$ be its public-coin randomized communication complexity with error $1/3$. Splitting $[n]$ into blocks $\{3j,3j+1,3j+2\}$, with Alice applying $(3j\ 3j{+}1)$ iff $x_j=1$ and Bob applying $(3j{+}1\ 3j{+}2)$ iff $y_j=1$, embeds disjointness on $\lfloor n/3\rfloor$ bits, so $\Omega(n)\le R(\mathrm{COMM}_n)\le O(n\log n)$. Determine $R(\mathrm{COMM}_n)$ up to a constant factor. In particular, decide whether $R(\mathrm{COMM}_n)=o(n\log n)$.

## Does the product of two permutations fix a point? | 4 | Computation
W: Alice holds a permutation π and Bob holds a permutation σ of n items. They want to know whether π after σ leaves some item where it was. A three-item gadget turns set disjointness into this problem, so about n bits are needed. Is about n bits always enough, or do they really need n log n?
M: Let $\mathrm{FIX}_n(\pi,\sigma)=1$ iff there is $i\in[n]$ with $\pi(\sigma(i))=i$. On each block of three points, Alice choosing $\mathrm{id}$ or a 3-cycle $c$, and Bob choosing $c^{-1}$ or $c^{-2}$ according to their bits (so that the block contributes a fixed point exactly when both bits are 1) embeds disjointness, giving $R(\mathrm{FIX}_n)=\Omega(n)$, while $R(\mathrm{FIX}_n)=O(n\log n)$ is trivial. Determine whether $R(\mathrm{FIX}_n)=\Theta(n)$, where $R$ is public-coin randomized communication complexity with error $1/3$.

## Information-tight sorting of pattern classes | 5 | Computation
W: Suppose an array is promised to avoid some fixed pattern (for example, no four entries in the relative order 1, 3, 2, 4). Counting the allowed orders gives a lower bound on the number of comparisons needed to sort it, and Fredman's theorem says this bound plus 2n comparisons always suffice. Is the counting bound always tight up to a negligible error, for every forbidden pattern?
M: For a permutation $\tau$ let $\mathrm{Av}_n(\tau)$ be the set of $\tau$-avoiding permutations of $[n]$ and let $C_\tau(n)$ be the least worst-case number of comparisons $a_i\overset{?}{<}a_j$ needed by a decision tree that determines the order of $a_1,\dots,a_n$ when the order pattern of $(a_i)$ is promised to lie in $\mathrm{Av}_n(\tau)$. Fredman's bound gives $\log_2|\mathrm{Av}_n(\tau)|\le C_\tau(n)\le\log_2|\mathrm{Av}_n(\tau)|+2n$. Decide whether $C_\tau(n)=\log_2|\mathrm{Av}_n(\tau)|+o(n)$ for every $\tau$. In particular, decide this for $\tau=1324$, whose growth rate is unknown.

## Erdős–Szekeres by comparisons | 4 | Computation
W: Any list of k² + 1 distinct numbers contains k + 1 numbers that rise or fall in order. How many comparisons between entries does it take to actually find such a run? Patience sorting uses about N log k comparisons for a list of N = k² + 1 numbers. Can it be done with a constant number of comparisons per entry?
M: Let $N=k^2+1$ and let $T(k)$ be the least worst-case number of comparisons $a_i\overset{?}{<}a_j$ needed by a deterministic algorithm that, given distinct reals $a_1,\dots,a_N$, outputs indices $i_1<\dots<i_{k+1}$ with $(a_{i_t})$ monotone. Patience sorting gives $T(k)=O(N\log k)$. Decide whether $T(k)=O(N)$ or $T(k)=\Theta(N\log k)$. Also determine the randomized (expected-comparison) complexity.

## Egyptian fractions with k terms | 5 | Computation
W: Given a fraction a/b and a number k, can a/b be written as a sum of k different unit fractions 1/x? Find out how hard this decision is. Even membership in NP is unclear, because the denominators can need exponentially many digits in k.
M: Let $\mathrm{EGYPT}=\{(a,b,1^k): \exists\,x_1<\dots<x_k\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\ a/b=\sum_{i=1}^k 1/x_i\}$, with $a,b$ in binary and $k$ in unary. Determine its complexity. Specifically: (i) is $\mathrm{EGYPT}\in\mathrm{NP}$? (ii) is it NP-hard, possibly under randomized reductions? (iii) is the restriction to $k=3$ decidable in time polynomial in $\log b$ given an oracle for integer factorization?

## Distances in four-peg Hanoi | 5 | Computation
W: In Tower of Hanoi with four pegs, the fewest moves between the two "all discs on one peg" positions is now known (Bousch, 2014). Give a polynomial-time method to find the fewest moves between any two legal positions, or show none exists under standard assumptions.
M: Let $H^4_n$ be the state graph of the Tower of Hanoi with 4 pegs and $n$ discs: vertices are $\{1,2,3,4\}^n$ (peg of each disc), and edges are legal single-disc moves. Is there an algorithm computing $d_{H^4_n}(u,v)$ for arbitrary $u,v\in\{1,2,3,4\}^n$ in time $\mathrm{poly}(n)$? (For 3 pegs this is linear time; for 4 pegs no subexponential algorithm is known to the author.)

## Fewest presses in Lights Out | 4 | Computation
W: In Lights Out on an n × n board, pressing a cell flips it and its four neighbours. Given a pattern, find the fewest presses that turn every light off. Solving is linear algebra, but the solutions form a family of size 2^d, and d can be a large fraction of n. Is there a polynomial-time method for the fewest presses?
M: Let $A_n=T_n\otimes I+I\otimes T_n+I$ over $\mathbb F_2$, where $T_n$ is the adjacency matrix of the path $P_n$. Given $n$ and $b\in\mathbb F_2^{n^2}$ in the column space of $A_n$, compute $\min\{|x|:A_nx=b\}$ (Hamming weight). Is this computable in time $\mathrm{poly}(n)$? The nullity $d(n)=\dim\ker A_n=\deg\gcd(p_n(x),p_n(x+1))$, with $p_0=1,\ p_1=x,\ p_{k+1}=xp_k+p_{k-1}$, is large for some $n$: computed $d(527)=350$ and $d(495)=320$. So brute force over the kernel takes time $2^{\Theta(n)}$ for infinitely many $n$.

## Smallest zero-one multiple | 5 | Computation
W: Every n has a multiple written only with the digits 0 and 1, with at most n digits. Can the shortest such multiple be found in time polynomial in the number of digits of n? Or is deciding whether one exists with at most d digits NP-hard?
M: For $n\ge1$ coprime to 10 let $L(n)$ be the least number of digits of a positive multiple of $n$ whose decimal digits lie in $\{0,1\}$, i.e. the least $d$ with $\sum_{i\in S}10^i\equiv0\pmod n$ for some nonempty $S\subseteq\{0,\dots,d-1\}$. Decide whether the language $\{(n,1^d):L(n)\le d\}$ ($n$ in binary) is NP-hard under randomized polynomial-time reductions, or whether $L(n)$ can be computed in time $\mathrm{poly}(\log n)$.
