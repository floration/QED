## Largest plus smallest neighbour factor | 6 | Dynamics
W: Add the largest prime factor of n to the smallest prime factor of n + 1. Tested starts reach 5 → 7 → 9 or 22 → 34. Prove all do.
M: For $f(n)=P(n)+p(n+1)$, with $p$ the least prime factor, prove every orbit with $n\ge3$ reaches $(5,7,9)$ or $(22,34)$.

## Sum of prime divisors of neighbours | 6 | Dynamics
W: Add the sum of the distinct prime factors of n to that of n + 1. Tested starts reach 33 or loops through 10, 17, 30 or 107. Prove there is nothing else.
M: For $f(n)=\mathrm{sopf}(n)+\mathrm{sopf}(n+1)$, prove every orbit reaches $\{33\}$, $(10,18,24)$, $(17,22,36,42,55,25,20)$, $(30,41,53,58,90)$ or $(107,112,122)$.

## Mixed divisor-sum map | 6 | Dynamics
W: Add the sum of distinct prime factors of n to the largest prime factor of n + 1. Tested starts reach 14, 21, or loops through 5, 10, 31 or 82. Prove these are the only fates.
M: For $f(n)=\mathrm{sopf}(n)+P(n+1)$, prove every orbit reaches $\{14\},\{21\},(5,8),(10,18,24),(31,33)$ or $(82,126,139,146)$.

## Lopsided neighbour map | 6 | Dynamics
W: Map n to P(n) + 2P(n+1). Except for the fixed point 8, every tested start from 3 upward falls into 23 → 29 → 39. Prove this for all starts.
M: For $f(n)=P(n)+2P(n+1)$, prove every orbit with $n\ge3$, $n\ne8$, reaches $(23,29,39)$.

## The parity-switch prime map | 6 | Dynamics
W: For even n add P(n) and P(n+1); for odd n add P(n) and P(n−1). Every tested start falls into one nine-step loop through 5. Prove all do.
M: For $f(n)=P(n)+P(n+(-1)^n)$, prove every orbit with $n\ge3$ reaches $(5,7,10,16,19,22,34,24,8)$.

## The shift-6 prime map | 6 | Dynamics
W: Map n to P(n) + P(n+6). Tested starts reach 6 or the loop 8 ↔ 9. Prove all do.
M: For $f(n)=P(n)+P(n+6)$, prove every orbit with $n\ge3$ reaches $\{6\}$ or $(8,9)$.

## Factor plus next divisor-sum | 6 | Dynamics
W: Map n to P(n) plus the sum of distinct primes dividing n + 1. Every tested start falls into 16 → 19 → 26. Prove all do.
M: For $f(n)=P(n)+\mathrm{sopf}(n+1)$, prove every orbit with $n\ge2$ reaches $(16,19,26)$.

## Density of the basin of 22 ↔ 34 | 6 | Dynamics
W: For n ↦ P(n) + p(n+1), about 6% of tested starts reach the 22 ↔ 34 loop. Show the fraction has a limit and it is positive.
M: For $f(n)=P(n)+p(n+1)$, prove $\lim_{x}\frac1x\#\{n\le x:\text{orbit reaches }(22,34)\}$ exists and is positive.

## Growth rate of square-sum Hamiltonian cycles | 7 | Combinatorics
W: Find the exponential growth rate of the number of ways to arrange 1 to N in a circle with neighbours summing to squares.
M: Determine $\lim_N\frac{\log H(N)}{N\log N}$ where $H(N)$ counts Hamiltonian cycles of $S_N$, and prove the limit exists.

## Permanent of the prime-sum matrix | 6 | Combinatorics
W: Find the asymptotic count of perfect matchings between odd and even numbers up to 2n whose sums are all prime.
M: Determine $\log\mathrm{per}(M_n)$ to $o(n)$ where $M_n=(\mathbf 1[2i-1+2j\text{ prime}])_{i,j\le n}$.

## Square-sum matchings count | 6 | Combinatorics
W: Count the ways to pair up 1 to N into pairs summing to squares. Find the exponential growth.
M: Determine $\lim_N\frac{\log m(N)}{N}$ (or the right normalisation) for the number $m(N)$ of perfect matchings of $S_N$, $N$ even.

## Optimal square-sum circle gaps | 5 | Combinatorics
W: Among all arrangements of 1 to N in a circle where neighbours sum to squares, minimise the largest difference between neighbours. Find the order of the minimum.
M: Determine the order of $\min_{C}\max_{i}|v_{i+1}-v_i|$ over Hamiltonian cycles $C$ of $S_N$.

## Primes on circles centred at the origin | 7 | Number theory
W: Find the largest number of prime points (p, q) lying on a single circle centred at the origin with radius up to R.
M: Determine the order of $\max_{n\le R^2}\#\{(p,q)\in\mathbb{P}^2:p^2+q^2=n\}$.

## Continued fraction of the P-constant | 8 | Number theory
W: Show the constant formed by largest prime factors as continued fraction digits is transcendental.
M: Prove $[0;P(2),P(3),P(4),\dots]$ is transcendental.

## Thue–Morse of P(n) and P(n+1) | 7 | Number theory
W: Show the Thue–Morse values at P(n) and P(n+1) are uncorrelated.
M: Prove $\sum_{n\le x}(-1)^{s_2(P(n))+s_2(P(n+1))}=o(x)$.
