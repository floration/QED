## Area-bisector polygon map | 4 | Geometry
W: From each corner of a convex polygon draw the line that cuts its area in half, and join the points where these lines leave the polygon to get a new polygon. Repeating this does not make polygons regular: tested pentagons up to nonagons all freeze, after about a hundred steps, into irregular shapes that the map only stretches and shears. Prove this always happens, and find how many free parameters the frozen shapes have.
M: For a convex $n$-gon $P=v_1\cdots v_n$ ($n\ge5$) let $w_i\neq v_i$ be the second boundary point of the unique line through $v_i$ halving the area of $P$, and $B(P)=\mathrm{conv}(w_1,\dots,w_n)$ (the $w_i$ are in cyclic order). Prove that for every convex $n$-gon the affine classes of $B^k(P)$ converge as $k\to\infty$, the limit $Q$ satisfying $B(Q)=A(Q)$ for an affine map $A$ (up to a cyclic shift of labels). Determine the dimension of the set of such affine classes $Q$ inside the $(2n-6)$-dimensional space of affine classes of $n$-gons. (Numerics, $5\le n\le9$, random starts: convergence to non-affine-regular limits that depend on the start, so this set has positive dimension.)

## Most uniform chords | 5 | Geometry
W: Cut a convex shape by a random line. The disc seems to make the chord length vary least compared with its average: its ratio of mean square chord to squared mean chord is 32/(3π²) ≈ 1.081, against 1.132 for the hexagon, 1.205 for the square and 1.336 for the triangle. Prove no convex shape beats the disc.
M: For a planar convex body $K$ with perimeter $L$ and area $A$, let $\sigma$ be the chord length of a random line under the motion-invariant measure conditioned on meeting $K$, so $\mathbb E\sigma=\pi A/L$ and $\mathbb E\sigma^2=\frac1L\iint_{K\times K}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|}$. Prove $L\iint_{K\times K}|x-y|^{-1}dx\,dy\ \ge\ \tfrac{32}{3}A^2$, with equality only for discs. (Numerically checked for regular 3-, 4-, 6-gons and for small $\cos 2\theta$, $\cos3\theta$ radial perturbations of the disc. Riesz rearrangement and the isoperimetric inequality push in opposite directions.)

## Rolling polygons | 6 | Geometry
W: Take a polygon and, going round its corners in order, reflect each corner across the line through its two neighbours; side lengths never change, so the shape tumbles across the plane. Quadrilaterals march off in a straight line, but tested pentagons, hexagons and heptagons wander far more slowly, roughly like a random walk. Prove that for five or more sides the drift is slower than linear, and decide whether it is genuinely diffusive.
M: For a planar closed polygon $(v_1,\dots,v_n)$ apply, for $s=1,2,\dots$ with $i\equiv s\pmod n$, the move $v_i\mapsto$ reflection of $v_i$ in the line $v_{i-1}v_{i+1}$ (a half-turn bending in the sense of Kapovich–Millson); it preserves all side lengths. Let $c_N$ be the vertex centroid after $N$ moves. For $n=4$, $|c_N|/N$ tends to a nonzero limit for generic starts. Prove that for $n\ge5$ and Lebesgue-almost every initial polygon, $c_N/N\to0$. Decide whether $|c_N-c_0|=N^{1/2+o(1)}$ for almost every start. (Tested: $n=5,6,7$, a few random near-regular starts, $N\le3\cdot10^5$: displacements $\approx10^2$ at $N=3\cdot10^5$, versus $\approx0.17N$ for $n=4$.)

## Equal-area realizations of polyhedra | 5 | Geometry
W: Can every type of convex polyhedron, specified by which faces meet which, be built so that all its faces have exactly the same area? Pyramids, prisms and tetrahedra can. Find a polyhedron type that cannot be, or show all can.
M: Does every 3-connected planar graph $G$ have a realization as a convex 3-polytope whose 2-faces all have equal area? If not, characterize the graphs that do. (The realization space modulo similarities has dimension $e(G)-1$ and equal areas impose $f(G)-1$ conditions, so the expected dimension is $e-f=v-2$; decide whether every nonempty equiareal realization space has this dimension.)

## Cubic unit distances | 6 | Geometry
W: Call two points a cubic pair if the second is reached from the first by a step of the form (t, t³). Among n points in the plane, how many cubic pairs can there be? The general theory caps it at about n^(4/3), while a stretched grid gives about n^(5/4). Find the true exponent.
M: Let $\Gamma=\{(t,t^3):t\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\}$ and $U(n)=\max_{|P|=n}\#\{(p,q)\in P^2:\ q-p\in\Gamma\}$. Translates of $\Gamma$ pairwise meet in at most two points and form a two-parameter family, so $U(n)=O(n^{4/3})$; the set $\{1,\dots,N\}\times\{1,\dots,N^3\}$ gives $U(n)=\Omega(n^{5/4})$. Determine $\lim \log U(n)/\log n$, or at least prove $U(n)=O(n^{4/3-c})$ for some $c>0$, or $U(n)=\Omega(n^{5/4+c})$. (For the parabola $(t,t^2)$ the analogous count is $\Theta(n^{4/3})$, as in Valtr's strictly convex norms; the question is whether the curvature-vanishing inflection of $t^3$ at the origin and its cubic growth change the exponent.)

## Only the sphere is of negative type | 6 | Geometry
W: Distances measured along the surface of a round ball pass Schoenberg's "negative type" test, one of the basic conditions for a distance to come from a sum of cut-and-measure pieces. Distances along the surface of a cube fail it, and so do distances on a flat doubled disc. Show that the round sphere is the only convex surface whose surface distances pass.
M: For a convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^3$ let $d_K$ be the intrinsic (geodesic) metric of $\partial K$. Call $d_K$ of negative type if $\sum_{i,j}b_ib_j\,d_K(x_i,x_j)\le0$ for all finite $x_i\in\partial K$ and real $b_i$ with $\sum b_i=0$. Round spheres qualify ($d_K$ is even $L^1$-embeddable, by Crofton's formula). Prove that if $d_K$ is of negative type then $K$ is a ball. Allow degenerate (doubly covered planar) $K$. (Computed: for $84$ points on the unit cube surface, including the vertices, the matrix $(I-\tfrac1mJ)D(I-\tfrac1mJ)$ has eigenvalue $0.366>0$; for $120$ random points on a doubly covered unit disc it has eigenvalue $3.0>0$.) Is the same true with negative type replaced by $L^1$-embeddability, and does some neighbourhood of the round sphere already fail?

## How round is the cube's surface? | 6 | Geometry
W: Distances along the surface of a cube can be copied into a straight-line space with every distance stretched or shrunk by a factor of at most D. A belt around the cube is an exact circle, which forces D to be at least π/2, while the cube's own position in space gives D = 2. Find the best D, and decide whether it is exactly π/2.
M: Let $d$ be the intrinsic metric on the surface $S$ of the unit cube and $c_2(S)=\inf_f \mathrm{Lip}(f)\,\mathrm{Lip}(f^{-1})$ over injective $f:S\to\ell_2$. The equatorial belt is isometric to a circle of length $4$, so $c_2(S)\ge\pi/2$, and the inclusion $S\subset\mathbb R^3$ has distortion $2$ (opposite face centres). Determine $c_2(S)$; in particular decide whether $c_2(S)=\pi/2$. (Checked numerically: on 400 random pairs of belt points the surface distance equals the belt distance.) Determine also the value for the regular octahedron.

## Fattest cyclic polytopes | 4 | Geometry
W: Put n points on the curve (t, t², t³) with t between −1 and 1, both endpoints included, and choose them to make the convex hull as big as possible. In the plane, on the parabola, equal spacing is best. In three and four dimensions the best points crowd toward the ends, and they are not the zeros of any classical orthogonal polynomial. Find the limiting density of the best points.
M: For $d\ge3$ and $n>d$ let $V_d(n)=\max \mathrm{vol}_d\,\mathrm{conv}\{\gamma(t_1),\dots,\gamma(t_n)\}$ over $-1=t_1<\dots<t_n=1$, with $\gamma(t)=(t,t^2,\dots,t^d)$. Prove that the empirical measures of the maximizing nodes converge weakly to a density $\rho_d$ on $[-1,1]$, determine $\rho_d$ (it is uniform for $d=2$), and find the exponent $\alpha_d$ in $\mathrm{vol\,conv}\,\gamma([-1,1])-V_d(n)\asymp n^{-\alpha_d}$. (Numerical optima for $d=3,4$, $n\le8$ cluster at the ends, e.g. $d=3,n=8$: $\pm0.7667,\pm0.4978,\pm0.1785$, and they match no Gauss–Jacobi node set. The deficit is not a sum of local gap terms, because by Gale's evenness condition facets join non-adjacent nodes.)

## Equilateral associahedra | 5 | Geometry
W: The permutohedron and the cube can be built with every edge the same length. Can the associahedron, the polytope whose vertices are the bracketings of a product, also be built as a convex polytope with all edges equal, in every dimension?
M: Does the $d$-dimensional associahedron $K_{d+2}$ have, for every $d\ge3$, a convex realization in $\mathbb R^d$ (any realization with the correct face lattice) all of whose edges have the same length? Same question for the cyclohedron. If not, find the first $d$ that fails. The standard realizations (Loday, Chapoton–Fomin–Zelevinsky) are not equilateral.

## Triangle areas determine a polygon | 5 | Geometry
W: Write down the areas of all triangles formed by three corners of a convex polygon, as an unlabelled list. Does the list determine the polygon up to area-preserving affine maps? Prove this for generic polygons, and decide whether two genuinely different convex polygons can ever have the same list.
M: For a convex $n$-gon $P$ let $\mathcal A(P)$ be the multiset of the $\binom n3$ areas of triangles with vertices among those of $P$. Prove that for every $n\ge5$ there is a dense open set of convex $n$-gons $P$ such that $\mathcal A(Q)=\mathcal A(P)$ for a convex $n$-gon $Q$ implies $Q=g(P)$ for some affine $g$ with $|\det g|=1$. Decide whether there exist, for some $n$, convex $n$-gons $P,Q$ with $\mathcal A(P)=\mathcal A(Q)$ that are not equi-affinely equivalent. This is the area analogue of the Boutin–Kemper reconstruction of point sets from unlabelled distances, where non-reconstructible (homometric) examples exist.
