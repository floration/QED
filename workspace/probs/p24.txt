## Area-bisector polygon map | 4 | Geometry
W: From each corner of a convex polygon draw the line that cuts its area in half, and join the points where these lines leave the polygon to get a new polygon. Repeating this does not make polygons regular: tested pentagons up to nonagons all freeze, after about a hundred steps, into irregular shapes that the map only stretches and shears. Prove this always happens, and find how many free parameters the frozen shapes have.
M: For a convex $n$-gon $P=v_1\cdots v_n$ ($n\ge5$) let $w_i\neq v_i$ be the second boundary point of the unique line through $v_i$ halving the area of $P$, and $B(P)=\mathrm{conv}(w_1,\dots,w_n)$ (the $w_i$ are in cyclic order). Prove that for every convex $n$-gon the affine classes of $B^k(P)$ converge as $k\to\infty$, the limit $Q$ satisfying $B(Q)=A(Q)$ for an affine map $A$ (up to a cyclic shift of labels). Determine the dimension of the set of such affine classes $Q$ inside the $(2n-6)$-dimensional space of affine classes of $n$-gons. (Numerics, $5\le n\le9$, random starts: convergence to non-affine-regular limits that depend on the start, so this set has positive dimension.)

## Most uniform chords | 5 | Geometry
W: Cut a convex shape by a random line. The disc seems to make the chord length vary least compared with its average: its ratio of mean square chord to squared mean chord is 32/(3π²) ≈ 1.081, against 1.132 for the hexagon, 1.205 for the square and 1.336 for the triangle. Prove no convex shape beats the disc.
M: For a planar convex body $K$ with perimeter $L$ and area $A$, let $\sigma$ be the chord length of a random line under the motion-invariant measure conditioned on meeting $K$, so $\mathbb E\sigma=\pi A/L$ and $\mathbb E\sigma^2=\frac1L\iint_{K\times K}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|}$. Prove $L\iint_{K\times K}|x-y|^{-1}dx\,dy\ \ge\ \tfrac{32}{3}A^2$, with equality only for discs. (Numerically checked for regular 3-, 4-, 6-gons and for small $\cos 2\theta$, $\cos3\theta$ radial perturbations of the disc. Riesz rearrangement and the isoperimetric inequality push in opposite directions.)
