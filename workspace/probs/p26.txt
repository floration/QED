## Shared cookie stacks | 5 | Probability
W: Two walkers take turns stepping on the integers and share a stack of M cookies at every site: whoever stands on a site with cookies left eats one and steps right with probability p, otherwise the step is fair. One walker alone escapes to the right exactly when M(2p − 1) > 1. Find the exact condition for both walkers to escape; is it M(2p − 1) > 2?
M: Fix $M\ge1$, $p\in(\tfrac12,1)$ and put $\delta=M(2p-1)$. Walkers $X^1,X^2$ start at $0$ and every site $x\in\mathbb Z$ starts with $c_x=M$ cookies. At time $t=1,2,\dots$ walker $i\equiv t\pmod 2$ moves: if it stands at $x$ with $c_x>0$ then $c_x\leftarrow c_x-1$ and it steps $+1$ with probability $p$, $-1$ otherwise; if $c_x=0$ it steps $\pm1$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each. For a single walker, $X_t\to+\infty$ a.s. iff $\delta>1$ (Kosygina–Zerner). Prove that the event $\{X^1_t\to+\infty,\ X^2_t\to+\infty\}$ has probability $0$ or $1$, that which case occurs depends only on $\delta$, and determine the threshold $\delta_c(2)$; in particular decide whether $\delta_c(2)=2$.

## Straight through the known | 6 | Probability
W: A walker on the square grid picks a fresh random direction every time it stands on a site for the first time, and otherwise just keeps going straight. So it moves ballistically through its own past and diffuses only at its frontier. How fast does the set of visited sites grow, and is it a solid blob or a fractal one?
M: Let $X_0=0$ and $X_{t+1}=X_t+d_t$, where $d_t$ is uniform on $\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2\}$ and independent of the past if $X_t\notin\{X_0,\dots,X_{t-1}\}$, and $d_t=d_{t-1}$ otherwise. Let $R_n=|\{X_0,\dots,X_n\}|$ and $D_n=\max_{t\le n}\|X_t\|_\infty$. Prove that $R_n=n^{\alpha+o(1)}$ and $D_n=n^{\beta+o(1)}$ almost surely for constants $\alpha,\beta$, determine them, and decide whether $\alpha=2\beta$ (the range has positive density in a ball of radius $D_n$). Tested: in three simulated runs of $1.05\cdot10^9$ steps, $R_n$ was between $4.8\cdot10^7$ and $5.1\cdot10^7$ and every visited site had both coordinates below $7000$ in absolute value; over the last factor 4 in time, $R_n$ grew by factors $2.7$ to $3.1$.

## Hamiltonian Delaunay triangulations | 6 | Probability
W: Delaunay triangulations are not always Hamiltonian, though they are 1-tough and close to it. Is the Delaunay triangulation of n random points in a square Hamiltonian with probability tending to one?
M: Let $X_1,\dots,X_n$ be i.i.d. uniform in $[0,1]^2$ and $D_n$ their Delaunay triangulation. Decide whether $P(D_n\text{ has a Hamiltonian cycle})\to1$; if not, decide whether it tends to $0$. (A positive density of local configurations has separating triangles, so Whitney's theorem does not apply directly; a local obstruction occurring with positive density would force the probability to $0$.)

## Two colours of a random 3-colouring | 6 | Probability
W: Colour the square grid with three colours uniformly at random so neighbours differ, and keep only the sites of colours 1 and 2. Along any path in this set the underlying height function is confined to two consecutive values. Does this set have an infinite connected component?
M: Let $c$ be a uniformly random proper 3-colouring of $\mathbb Z^2$ (the zero-tilt limit of uniform colourings of $n\times n$ tori), equivalently $c=h\bmod3$ for the uniform graph homomorphism height function $h:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z$. Decide whether $\{x:c(x)\ne3\}$ has an infinite nearest-neighbour cluster with positive probability; if not, determine the decay exponent of $P(0\leftrightarrow\partial[-r,r]^2)$ within this set. (Colour 3 is an independent set, so it can only block along diagonal chains; by colour symmetry the answer is the same for every pair of colours.)
