239 Open Problems

New mathematical problems for anyone to try. Each was checked against the literature and rated twice: once by its author, and once by independent raters who judged difficulty without trying to solve it.

Ratings, literature tags, and how to share a solution
  • Scale. 0: trivial. 1: graduate coursework. 5: the current edge of mathematics. 10: likely never solved, even by vastly greater intelligence.
  • Two ratings. The author's rating (whole numbers 1–10, designed as a bell curve around 6) and an adversarial rating (0.0–10.0) from separate AI raters. They never saw the author's ratings, were told to be skeptical, and didn't try to solve anything. Their flags mark problems they think are easy, a finite computation, ill-posed, or equivalent to a famous problem. Switch which rating drives the histogram, the colours and the sort with the toggle.
  • Curation, 24 Nov 2026. Every problem was scored 0–10 for novelty, importance and value by independent reviewers. Repeats, low-value problems (value below 4) and excess problems from over-represented themes (largest-prime-factor maps, sofa variants, hat/spectre transplants, square-sum graphs, Prime Nim, seven-free digits) were moved to an archive; 239 remain, including 48 new problems in topology, PDE and mathematical physics. Problem ids are permanent.
  • Expansion, 23 Nov 2026. 117 new problems added after a strict adversarial audit (63 of 180 drafts rejected as duplicate, false, easy, known, ill-posed or famous-equivalent); new problems average 5.2 on the calibrated scale.
  • Revision, 23 Nov 2026. I replaced 218 problems because they were false, already solved, already known, a famous open problem in disguise, trivial, or vague. Every replacement went through a blind adversarial rating, and replacements flagged as flawed were themselves replaced, over several rounds.
  • Literature tags come from a web search on 23 Nov 2026. Already solved: the answer is in print. Known problem: the literature already poses this question or something very close. Prior work: the problem is new, but there is directly relevant work to start from. Untagged problems turned up nothing close. That is evidence, not proof.
  • Each problem has a plain-words note and a precise math statement. If they seem to disagree, the math statement is the official one. Cite a problem by its ID, such as N-217.
  • Objects defined just for this list: LF-Collatz: f(n)=n/2 for even n, otherwise the largest prime factor of 3n+1. SOF Collatz: the same with the smallest odd prime factor. Mod-3 map: n/3, 2n+1 or 2n−1 by n mod 3. R(n): reverse the digits of n². Prime Nim: take a prime number of stones. SN, CN: numbers up to N joined when their sum is a square or a cube. Doubled twins: p, p+2, 2p+1, 2p+3 all prime. "Seven-free": no digit 7 in base 10. Disproving a problem counts as solving it.
